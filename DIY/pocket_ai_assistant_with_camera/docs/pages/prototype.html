Pocket AI Assistant · camera build

The tower, turning to face you

A concept view of the final build: the wire-cage tower, 45 × 42 × 115 mm, with the head (screen, camera, speaker) turning on the gear motor to follow whoever it sees. Drag the slider to walk around it. Not CAD, not built; the wiring is on the Pocket AI with Camera sheet.

Next to the first build

Same depth, a quarter narrower, three times taller: 45 × 42 × 115 mm against 60 × 42 × 35 mm. About two and a half times the volume, most of it the standing cell and the motor.

How it follows you

1 · see
The camera streams 240 × 240 frames into PSRAM. Face detection runs on the S3 itself (Espressif's ESP-WHO models), a few frames a second, no cloud.
2 · measure
The face's centre against the frame's centre is the error. Inside a ±5° dead band the head holds still, so it does not twitch while you talk.
3 · nudge
Outside it, the firmware pulses IN1 or IN2 (PWM on GPIO7/8) for 60-120 ms at a time and looks again. At 16 rpm a 100 ms pulse is about 10°.
4 · know where it is
At boot the head drives to the home stop on GPIO9 and counts from there; it re-homes whenever the count and the camera disagree. Travel is ±90° so the six neck wires never wind up.
5 · rest
No face for thirty seconds: the head returns to centre, the eyes idle, the camera drops to one frame a second. That is most of the battery saving.

What you would see

  • A taller, narrower cage. Two stacked loops-and-posts frames in the owned 1.5 mm brass or the 14-gauge nickel silver, soldered as before. The head is a separate cage on a short neck; nothing structural crosses the joint.
  • Bare-rod wiring, as before. Inside the head and inside the body every connection is 1.0 mm rod at height levels, the way the first harness was built. Only the six connections through the neck are flexible wire, because the head turns.
  • The face. The 0.96" OLED with Pip's eyes, the camera lens above it, the speaker firing down out of the head's floor. The USB-C socket is at the back of the head.
  • The body. The 16340 cell standing in a folded sleeve on the left, the motor on the right with its shaft up through the body's roof, the driver board at the bottom, the toggle switch at the back. The action button and the home stop sit on the body's roof at the front.
  • The finish. Bare nickel silver, white-painted brass, or bare brass; the buttons above switch the render. The cell sleeve stays metal either way, as decided for the first build.
What this page is not

A concept rendering from the target dimensions, not a measured model. Every module is calipered before the frame is cut, the same way the first build did it. Nothing has been built or powered.
