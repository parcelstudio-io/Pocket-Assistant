# Wiring

The net list, the pin audit, the ladder, the current budget and the meter checks for the camera build, Rev B (2026-11-10).
Drawn as `pages/wiring-board.html` (PDF in `pages/print/`). Generated by `pages/gen/wiring_board.py`; edit the script.
Nothing here has been built or measured; every figure is from the Seeed wiki, the MAX98357 datasheet or a vendor listing.

## Pin map

| Header | GPIO | Net | Job |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| D0 | 1 | `BCLK` | I2S bit clock to the amp |
| D1 | 2 | `LRC` | I2S word clock to the amp |
| D2 | 3 | `DIN` | I2S data to the amp |
| D3 | 4 | `AMP_SD` | amp shutdown, pull-down R1 keeps it silent at boot |
| D4 | 5 | `SDA` | I2C SDA, OLED |
| D5 | 6 | `IN3` | tilt motor IN3 (LEDC PWM), pull-down R7 |
| D6 | 43 | `SCL` | I2C SCL, OLED. UART0 TX chatters here at boot: clock pulses with SDA held high are not a start condition, so the screen ignores them |
| D7 | 44 | `IN4` | tilt motor IN4 (LEDC PWM), pull-down R8. UART0 RX is an input at boot; R8 holds the driver input low |
| D8 | 7 | `IN1` | pan motor IN1 (LEDC PWM), pull-down R2 |
| D9 | 8 | `IN2` | pan motor IN2 (LEDC PWM), pull-down R3 |
| D10 | 9 | `LADDER` | the ladder: ADC1 channel 8 reads which of the action button, the pan stop and the tilt stop is pressed |

Reserved by the Sense expansion board, never on the sheet:

- camera: GPIO 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 38, 39, 40, 47, 48
- pdm mic: GPIO 41, 42
- sd slot (empty): GPIO 7, 8, 9, 21

The script asserts that no header pin on the sheet collides with the camera or the microphone, that D8-D10
(the SD slot's SPI) are the only overlap and the slot stays empty, that the only strapping pin in use is
GPIO3, which does nothing on a stock XIAO, that the ladder sits on an ADC1 pin, and that every ladder state
is separable with 5 % resistors and 30 mV of ADC error.

## The ladder, state by state

| Pressed | Volts at D10 |
| --- | ---: |
| none | 3.30 V |
| action button | 0.21 V |
| pan stop | 0.60 V |
| tilt stop | 1.18 V |
| pan stop + tilt stop | 0.45 V |
| action button + pan stop | 0.16 V |
| action button + tilt stop | 0.19 V |
| action button + pan stop + tilt stop | 0.15 V |

Bands in the firmware: under 0.33 V the button (any combination), 0.33-0.52 V both stops, 0.52-0.85 V the pan stop,
0.85-1.6 V the tilt stop, above 2 V nothing. The button is ignored while homing.

## Why

### One cell, no boost

Every part on this sheet accepts 3.0-4.2 V: the XIAO has its own regulator and charger, the MX1508 runs from 2 V, the MAX98357A from 2.5 V, the OLED sits on the XIAO's 3.3 V. So the design has one rail, the cell itself. The PowerBoost, the 5 V harness points and the load-sharing worry of the first build all go away.

### Eyes first, then the head

The camera sees about 65° across; the eyes drawn on the 0.96 inch screen can only look about 20° off centre before they run out of glass. So the firmware finds the face in the frame and slides the eyes toward it first. When the face drifts past the eyes' reach, the head turns on its motors until the face is centred again and the eyes settle back to the middle. Eyes jump, head follows a beat later: a saccade, the thing that makes a face read as alive. The camera and the screen share one head, so where the camera looks is where the eyes look, by construction; the difference you would otherwise build with a second gimbal is drawn in pixels.

### Two axes, two gear motors, one driver

Pan from the body, tilt inside the head. The MX1508 has two bridges, so the second motor costs two pins and two pull-downs, not a board. The tilt axis runs through the head's centre of mass, so the tilt motor holds no weight and the gearbox's own friction keeps the head where it was left. Both motors were on hand on 2026-11-03.

### Why the MX1508 and not the L298N

The L298N's bipolar outputs lose 2-3 V and its onboard regulator wants 7 V or more. From a single 16340 a real L298N would hand the motor about 1 V. The board the builder linked is the MX1508: MOSFET outputs, 2-10 V in, inputs that read 3.3 V as high. Four control pins, two bridges, and both are used now.

### Why a DC gear motor needs a home stop

A DC motor has no idea where it is. The 16 rpm gearbox makes timed moves repeatable (100 ms is about 10°), but drift adds up, so the firmware drives each axis to its stop at boot and whenever it is unsure: tilt first, face down, then pan to the left stop, then up and centre. One switch per axis. A hobby servo would do all of this in one three-wire part; it wants 5 V and is louder and jerkier, so the motors stand.

### Twelve signals, eleven pins: the ladder

The header has eleven pins. Three I2S, one amp shutdown, two I2C, four motor inputs: ten. The action button, the pan stop and the tilt stop would make thirteen, so the three share one ADC pin through a resistor ladder: a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V, and each switch pulls the pin down through its own resistor. Nothing pressed is 3.3 V; the button alone 0.21 V, the pan stop 0.60 V, the tilt stop 1.18 V, both stops 0.45 V, and any combination with the button under 0.25 V. The firmware reads a band, not a level, and ignores the button while it is homing. 5 % resistors and the ADC's own error move each reading by 50 mV; the gaps are 200 mV or more. The script asserts that.

### Why SCL sits on the UART pin

GPIO43 is UART0 TX, and the boot ROM prints on it for about a hundred milliseconds before the firmware runs. Rev A left it spare for that reason. Rev B needs the pin, so it carries the one signal that boot chatter cannot disturb: the I2C clock. An I2C start is SDA falling while SCL is high; with SDA idle on its pull-up, clock pulses alone are nothing to the SSD1306. Any S3 pin can be I2C through the GPIO matrix, and the console goes over USB, so nothing is lost. GPIO44 (UART0 RX) is an input at boot and drives the tilt motor's IN4 through its pull-down, which holds the driver input at 0.6 V, far under its threshold.

### Why the amp keeps its shutdown line

An easy way to find a pin would be to leave the amp's SD open and let it run whenever the cell is on. The MAX98357's maker says to shut the amp down before stopping its clocks, else the output can sit at DC across the speaker. So R1 and the SD line stay, the amp is off until the firmware raises SD, and the pin came from the ladder instead.

### Why the amp runs from BAT+ and the OLED from 3V3

The XIAO's 3.3 V regulator is good for 700 mA, and the S3 with the camera can take 370 mA of that on its own. The amp (250 mA peaks) and a motor (360 mA stalled) would push it over, so they take the cell directly, which both are rated for. The OLED's 20 mA rides on 3V3 as before.

### Charging through the XIAO, and its limit

The XIAO's charger is an SGM40567 set to 50-100 mA (the wiki says 100, the datasheet 50, owners report both). The device awake draws more than that, so with USB in and the switch ON the cell still goes down unless the firmware deep-sleeps. In-device charging is therefore an overnight trickle with the device asleep: 10-20 hours from empty. The charger has no thermistor input: room temperature only, first cycle watched. The practical path is to pull the cell and use an external 16340 charger (about 2 hours); the protected NL169 is made for that, and thirteen cells means one is always charged. That charger is the one optional item not yet owned.

### USB in with the switch OFF

The XIAO runs from USB for flashing and bench work with no cell. The amp stays off (R1 holds SD low) and the driver idles at under 1 mA, so the dead BAT+ rail is harmless. Never run a motor or the amp in this state: the rail has no cell behind it.

### The 5V pin

On the XIAO it is the USB VBUS, nothing more. On battery it reads 0 V. Nothing in this design touches it, and nothing ever feeds it.

### Bare rods where nothing moves, five wires where it does

The builder wires with bare rod, as the first harness did: the owned 1.0 mm brass, soldered to the header pins at height levels so no two nets touch. Inside the head and inside the body every connection on the sheet is a rod. The two joints are different: a rod cannot flex ten thousand times, so the five connections that cross them (BAT+ and GND down to the head in 26 AWG; the ladder and the tilt motor's pair in 30 AWG) are silicone wire, run up one yoke upright as a single line, with a loose coil round the pan hub and a loop at the tilt pin. The holder, the motors and the speaker keep their factory leads, and the XIAO's BAT pads on the back of the board get a 10 mm flexible stub each rather than a rod soldered to a surface pad. Resistance is not a concern: 50 mm of 1.0 mm brass is 4 mΩ, nothing at 1 A. The frame stays floating, so every rod keeps 2 mm from it, as before.

### Colour, under the rules of 2026-11-07 and 11-09

White, ivory or black as much as possible; the frame its own metal; no paint and no skins. The frame and the yoke are bare brass. The screen is white on black glass, the XIAO and its Sense board are black, the speaker, the holder and the switch body are black, the switch lever and the motors are bare metal, the wires that show are white or black from the owned kits, the heat-shrink is white. The parts that come in other colours are placed where they do not show: the driver (red or blue) behind the holder in the body, the amp under the speaker, the OLED's board (blue on some packs) behind its own glass, the cell's printed wrap inside the holder. The one owned part this build does not use for colour reasons is nothing: there is no part to replace.

### What the Sense board owns

Fourteen GPIOs for the camera, two for the PDM microphone, and the SD slot's SPI on GPIO7/8/9, which are the header's D8-D10. Those three carry the pan motor and the ladder here, so the SD slot stays empty for good. That leaves exactly the eleven header pins on the sheet, all eleven in use.

## Current budget

| Load | Fed from | Typical | Peak | Figure from |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense, Wi-Fi + camera streaming | 3V3 regulator (inside the XIAO) | 155 mA | 366 mA | Seeed wiki, webcam example on battery |
| XIAO, Wi-Fi connected, camera idle | 3V3 regulator | 110 mA | 150 mA | Seeed wiki, Wi-Fi active |
| OLED, all pixels | 3V3 pin | 20 mA | 25 mA | SSD1306 module, typical |
| Amp + speaker at the cap of 65 | BAT+ rail | 60 mA | 250 mA | 0.47 W into 8 Ω at 4.2 V, 85 % efficient |
| Pan motor, running / stalled | BAT+ rail | 100 mA | 360 mA | 12 mm class; this listing publishes none: measure yours |
| Tilt motor, running / stalled | BAT+ rail | 100 mA | 360 mA | same motor; the firmware moves one axis at a time |

- 3V3 regulator: camera streaming 366 + OLED 25 ≈ 390 mA peak, inside the XIAO's 700 mA. The amp and the motors are not on it.
- Cell, worst second: 390 + 250 + 360 ≈ 1.0 A if a motor stalls while it talks and streams; one axis moves at a time; both stalled would be 1.36 A, under the NL169's 2 A trip.
- Cell, average: about 270 mA listening, talking and following with the camera on, so about 3 h; about 130 mA Wi-Fi idle, about 7 h; 3 mA in deep sleep.
- Charge: 50-100 mA through the XIAO's USB-C (SGM40567; Seeed wiki says 100, datasheet 50), so 10-20 h from empty and only with the device asleep. An external 16340 charger (optional, not owned) does it in about 2 h.
- Amp: volume cap 65 and GAIN open; at a full 4.2 V cell the worst case is (0.65 × 4.2)² / 2 / 8 Ω = 0.47 W into the 0.8 W speaker.
- Motors: rated 3 V, run at ≤ 70 % PWM so the average voltage stays under 3 V at a full cell.

## Meter checks before the first power-up

1. **Holder polarity.** Cell in, nothing else connected: meter DC V on the holder leads. Red lead must read +3.6 to +4.2 V. If it reads negative the leads are swapped; swap them at the holder, never at the pads. On the XIAO's back, the BAT− pad is the one nearer the USB-C socket.
2. **No shorts, unpowered.** Switch OFF, cell out. Ω between the BAT+ rail and GND: open or above 1 kΩ (the 220 µF makes it climb). 3V3 to GND: above 1 kΩ. BAT+ to 3V3: open.
3. **OLED silkscreen.** Hold the module next to the sheet and read the four pin names before you solder. Two vendors, two orders.
4. **Driver inputs.** Unpowered, Ω from each of IN1-IN4 to GND on the bare module. Under 100 kΩ means it has its own pull-downs and R2, R3, R7, R8 can go; open means keep them.
5. **The ladder, unpowered.** Ω from the S pin to GND with nothing pressed: open. Press the button: 680 Ω. The pan stop: 2.2 kΩ. The tilt stop: 5.6 kΩ. Both stops: 1.6 kΩ. If a reading is off by more than 10 % a resistor is in the wrong place.
6. **Bench supply first.** Holder empty. SKY TOPPOWER at 3.8 V, limit 0.5 A, + to the switch's 'in', − to GND. Switch ON: the XIAO boots, 3V3 pin reads 3.25-3.35 V, draw under 200 mA with the screen on. Then the camera stream: under 400 mA, no resets.
7. **The ladder, powered.** Meter DC V on D10: 3.3 V with nothing pressed, about 0.21 V on the button, 0.60 V on the pan stop, 1.18 V on the tilt stop, 0.45 V with both stops. The firmware's bands sit halfway between.
8. **Motors, head off the shafts.** Raise the limit to 1.5 A. Each motor in turn: 50 % PWM one way, then the other. Note the running current; stall it with a finger for one second and note the peak. Under 0.5 A stalled is the pass. Then with the head on: tilt down to the stop, pan to the stop, and watch that the wires coil and uncoil without pulling.
9. **First charge, watched.** Cell in, switch ON, USB in, device asleep. Meter in series with the cell's + lead on the mA range: expect roughly 100 mA (Seeed says 100; one datasheet says 50). Cell cooler than 45 °C by touch after twenty minutes. The red LED goes out when it is full; the first full charge is not left unattended.
10. **Speaker.** Volume cap 65 in the firmware, GAIN pin open. Scope the speaker differentially (A−B, both grounds on GND, never on a speaker lead): under 2.7 V peak.

## Firmware changes (none written yet)

- Target esp32s3; keep the cap of 65, amp off until SD (and SD low before the clocks stop), the tx-power cap as an option; console on USB-serial-JTAG.
- I2S standard TX on GPIO1/2/3, SD on GPIO4; I2S PDM RX on GPIO42 (clk) / GPIO41 (data) replaces the INMP441 driver; I2C on GPIO5/43.
- esp32-camera with the XIAO ESP32S3 Sense pin table (OV2640 and OV5640 both work); frames in PSRAM; the face box drives the eyes, and past ±20° the head.
- LEDC PWM on GPIO7/8 (pan) and GPIO6/44 (tilt) at 20 kHz, duty ≤ 70 %, one axis at a time; home tilt then pan at boot and on doubt.
- ADC1 ch 8 (GPIO9) reads the ladder at 100 Hz; bands at 0.33 / 0.52 / 0.85 / 1.6 V; button ignored while homing.
