# Pocket AI Assistant with Camera

The second Pocket AI: the same screen, amplifier and speaker as
[`pocket_ai_assistant`](../pocket_ai_assistant/), with a camera, a head that
turns and tilts, and a rechargeable cell. Started 2026-10-27 as a paper design
(Rev A, pan only). **Rev B, drawn 2026-11-10, adds the tilt axis; the builder approved it as
drawn the same day** (the Cyber Dawn speaker was considered and declined: the
small speaker stays in the head). Nothing here has been built, flashed or measured.

## What it is

A tower about 50 × 42 × 122 mm. The head sits in a U-shaped brass yoke and
tilts ±90° on a shaft through its centre; the yoke turns ±150° on the pan
motor's shaft in the body. The head carries everything that faces the world: a
**Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense** (camera, PDM microphone and a Li-ion charger on
one 21 × 18 mm stack), the owned SSD1306 OLED, the owned MAX98357A amplifier
and speaker, and the tilt gear motor, whose shaft is fixed to the yoke. The body
carries the heavy, still things: one **Nitecore NL169 16340** cell in a holder,
the power switch, the **MX1508** dual driver, the pan gear motor, the pan home
stop and the action button.

The eyes on the screen do the fine pointing: the firmware finds the face in the
camera frame (about 65° wide), slides the drawn eyes toward it, and only when
the face passes the eyes' reach (about 20°) does the head turn to re-centre it.
Camera and screen share one head, so the eyes always face where the camera
faces.

One cell is the only rail. Every part accepts 3.0-4.2 V, so there is no boost
converter, no 5 V harness and no PowerBoost. The XIAO charges the cell through
its own USB-C.

## The pin map (Rev B)

| Header | GPIO | Job |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| D0 / D1 / D2 | 1 / 2 / 3 | I2S BCLK / LRC / DIN to the amp |
| D3 | 4 | amp SD, 4.7 kΩ pull-down |
| D4 | 5 | I2C SDA, OLED |
| D5 | 6 | tilt motor IN3, 10 kΩ pull-down |
| D6 | 43 | I2C SCL, OLED (UART0 TX chatter at boot is harmless to a clock line) |
| D7 | 44 | tilt motor IN4, 10 kΩ pull-down |
| D8 / D9 | 7 / 8 | pan motor IN1 / IN2, 10 kΩ pull-downs |
| D10 | 9 | the ladder: action button (680 Ω), pan stop (2.2 kΩ), tilt stop (5.6 kΩ) on one ADC pin, 10 kΩ pull-up |

All eleven header pins are used. Owned by the Sense board and never touched:
camera on GPIO 10-18, 38-40, 47, 48; PDM mic on 41/42; the SD slot's SPI on
7/8/9 (which is why the slot stays empty) and its chip select on 21.

## Rules carried over from the first build

- The cell is the only source. Nothing is ever fed into the XIAO's `5V` pin,
  and nothing but the holder is ever connected to its BAT pads.
- The amplifier is off until the firmware raises `SD`, and `SD` goes low before
  the I2S clocks stop; the volume cap stays at 65 with `GAIN` open (0.47 W
  worst case at a full cell, against the speaker's 0.8 W).
- Neither speaker lead is ground: no GND wire, no scope ground, ever.
- The brass frame is not a conductor.
- Never solder to the cell or charge it below 0 °C. The first full charge
  through the XIAO is watched, not left overnight.
- A matching listing or a compiled firmware proves nothing about hardware. Say
  "untested" until it has run.

## Why these parts, in one paragraph each

**The XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense** replaces the ESP32-C3 SuperMini, the INMP441
microphone and the PowerBoost at once: camera, PDM mic, 8 MB PSRAM for frames,
a 700 mA 3.3 V regulator and a Li-ion charger, all on the header pins the
first build already used for I2C and I2S.

**The MX1508** is the board the builder linked, sold as an "L298N
replacement". It is a MOSFET bridge: 2-10 V motor supply, 1.5 A, inputs that
read 3.3 V as high, no logic-supply pin. A real L298N would need 7 V and lose
2-3 V in its outputs; from one cell it would give the motor about 1 V. Rev B
uses both of its bridges: A pans, B tilts.

**The two 16 rpm gear motors** are slow enough that timed moves are
repeatable (100 ms is about 10°). A DC motor does not know its angle, so each
axis has a tactile home stop the firmware returns to at boot: tilt face-down
first, then pan to the left. Both motors have been on the bench since
2026-11-03. Two hobby servos would do the same job in two three-wire parts;
they want 5 V and are louder, so the motors stand.

**The ladder** exists because the XIAO has eleven header pins and the build
needs twelve signals. The action button and the two stops share one ADC pin,
each through its own resistor; every combination reads a different voltage,
and the firmware reads bands.

**The NL169** is protected (2 A continuous, 4.20 V termination), so the XIAO's
plain charger suits it, and it swaps out for an external 16340 charger when
10 hours at 100 mA is too slow.
