Step 11: the device speaks, on USB power

The prototype is a USB-powered home device, so the speaker can run from the same supply as everything else. That removes the translator, regulator, switch, fuses and harness from the battery design. Every part on this page is already in your inventory. Nothing here has run on hardware yet.

Rules for this step
  • USB is the only source. The amplifier's VIN comes from the controller's 5V pin only, never from the bench supply, a battery, or the 3.3 V rail.
  • Neither speaker output is ground. Never connect OUT+ or OUT− to GND, and never clip a scope ground to either.
  • The first powered output drives the dummy load, never the speaker, unless you take the shortcut below at cap 15.
  • Unplug USB before every wiring change. You have six speakers, but never cut, strip or re-terminate the leads of one you intend to use.
Pass 11A · meter

Wired and silent. Diagnostics build, SD below 0.08 V, the rest of the prototype still passes.

Pass 11B · scope

Sound into the dummy load, measured. Filtered peak of OUT+ minus OUT− at or below 2.53 V.

Pass 11C · speaker

The device speaks at the cap that passed 11B, five replies without a reset.

Shortcut chosen on 22 September

By your decision, the first speaker test skips the dummy load and PASS 11B, at the default volume cap of 15. The datasheet puts the speaker's peak at no more than 0.24 V there, about a tenth of its 2.53 V limit, at any gain setting. The hour that does it, with every step drawn, is One hour: make A1 speak. The dummy load and PASS 11B still apply to caps above 30.

Why one supply is enough

The purchase authority designed its amplifier fixture for a battery device. There the controller can run from USB while the amplifier's rail is off. Clock and data lines driven into an unpowered chip push current backwards through its input protection diodes, so that design needed a translator to isolate the two sides. When the amplifier takes power from the controller's own 5V pin, both sides switch on and off together with the USB cable, and that state never happens.

What you need, all owned

  • The HiLetgo MAX98357A board AMP-A1, with its SD resistor network traced in the unpowered speaker lab.
  • The speaker SPK-A1, one of six.
  • Seven header pins from the spare 1×40 strip, plus the board's terminal block if it came with one.
  • One 220 µF electrolytic rated 10 V or more from the ALLECIN kit, and one 100 nF ceramic from the BOJACK kit.
  • One pull-down resistor for SD, and twelve 100 Ω resistors for the dummy load, from the resistor kit. Two short lengths of 26 AWG wire. Jumpers.

Do, unpowered

  1. Solder the headers. Practise two joints on scrap header first, then solder the seven pins, and the terminal block if there is one, to AMP-A1. Inspect every joint at phone macro zoom. Clear any bridge with the solder wick and a touch of flux, then inspect again.

  2. Choose the SD pull-down from your trace. If the board has a pull-up from SD to VIN, pick the kit value no larger than one hundredth of it and no smaller than 1 kΩ. If there is no pull-up, use 4.7 kΩ. Write both values down.

  1. Build the dummy load. Twelve 100 Ω resistors side by side between the two 26 AWG wires, each leg soldered to both wires. Measure it unpowered, subtract the lead resistance, and label it with the reading. If the kit has no 100 Ω, any value from 68 Ω to 120 Ω works; use the value divided by eight of them.

  1. Wire it, unpowered, as the table says, with OUT+ and OUT− left open for now. Place the two capacitors at the amplifier. Photograph the wiring.

  2. Check with the meter, still unpowered. VIN to the 5V pin and GND to GND are continuous. VIN to GND gives at most a brief chirp, never a steady tone. OUT+ and OUT− are not continuous with GND.

Do, after 11A and once the scope has passed its incoming check

  1. Build and verify the amplifier-enabled image. Continue only when the verifier and the preview both print amplifier: ENABLED (Step 11, USB-powered). This full flash clears saved Wi-Fi settings, so you will provision A1 again as in Step 10.

    . firmware/.work/esp-idf/export.sh
firmware/scripts/build.sh --assistant --amplifier
python3 firmware/scripts/verify_source_build.py
firmware/scripts/flash.sh /dev/ttyACM0 --dry-run

  2. Connect the dummy load to OUT+ and OUT− with USB unplugged. Flash and open the monitor. The log prints Prototype volume cap: 15; amplifier: qualified opt-in.

  3. Set up the scope. Channel 1 on OUT+, channel 2 on OUT−, both ground clips on the controller's GND. Turn on the 20 MHz bandwidth limit on both. Make a math trace of channel 1 minus channel 2 and apply the low-pass math at about 20 kHz, which removes the switching carrier that a speaker's coil would normally block.

  4. Capture three long replies with a single trigger each. Record the largest filtered peak of the difference trace.

The volume cap. The firmware squares the volume setting before scaling the audio, so the cap limits amplitude far more than its number suggests. The default of 15 is very safe and probably quiet. To go louder, add a line such as CONFIG_POCKET_AI_MAX_VOLUME=30 to firmware/sdkconfig.defaults, rebuild, and repeat items 6 to 9 at the new value before the speaker goes back on. Raise it in steps and stop at the first value whose peak approaches 2.53 V.

If the board resets during loud replies, suspect the USB source first: a laptop port may limit current. After flashing, a wall adapter rated 1 A or more can power the device, with the OLED as the only readout.

Do, after 11B

  1. Swap in the speaker. Unplug USB. Replace the dummy load with the speaker, one lead on OUT+ and one on OUT−, clamped gently. Plug USB back in and ask one question.
