Brass harness, step by step

Twelve moves to a working prototype. Nothing on this page is optional. The harness is built on a printed card that serves as the jig; when every rod is on, the harness is rigid, the card comes off, and the bare harness goes into the frame. Each rod has its drawing, its cut length and its bends; x is millimetres from the left wall, y from the front wall, heights are above the card.

Three rules
  • USB unplugged whenever the iron is on.
  • OUT+ and OUT− go to the speaker only — never to GND, a rail or the frame.
  • The frame touches no rod and no rail.

4 · Six channel rods

For each: sand the ends, bend to the table, hold with the helping hands, flux, 1–2 s of iron per joint. The straight rods lie against the short top stubs, 5 mm up, and pass 3.5 mm above the rails.

9 · Resistors

Two, from the kit; measure each before it goes in. Bend leads with the pliers next to the body, cut 3 mm past the joint. The GAIN resistor and the mic-SD pull-down were dropped on 2026-10-14: GAIN stays open (9 dB) and the firmware pulls GPIO4 down itself. R3 stays until a meter check of A1 (GPIO2 to 3V3, unpowered: about 10 kΩ means the board has its own pull-up and R3 can go too).
