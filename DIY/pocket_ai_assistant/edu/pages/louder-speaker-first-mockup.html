0:00Louder, step 1: cap 30

The image with cap 30 is already built and verified (SHA-256 starting 595a4c8b). Cap 30 plays four times the amplitude of cap 15, about 12 dB louder, and needs no dummy load.

  1. Plug A1's USB into the computer and tell Claude. The speaker can stay connected. A1 was unplugged when this page was last checked, so nothing has been flashed yet.

  2. Flash it, keeping your Wi-Fi. Or tell Claude it is plugged in, and Claude runs this for you.

    . firmware/.work/esp-idf/export.sh
firmware/scripts/flash.sh /dev/ttyACM0 --keep-wifi --monitor

  3. Find Prototype volume cap: 30 in the log, then leave the monitor with Ctrl+].

  4. Turn the volume up once. A1 saved volume 15, and a higher cap does not raise it. Press the button and say "set the volume to maximum". It goes to 30 and saves it.

0:15Louder, step 2: the GAIN pin

The amplifier has its own volume knob, set by what you connect to its GAIN pin. Yours is unconnected, which is the quietest common setting, 9 dB. One resistor raises it to 15 dB, another 6 dB louder. The datasheet arithmetic already allowed the highest gain at cap 30, so this stays under the speaker's limit.

  1. Unplug USB.

  2. Find a 100 kΩ in the resistor kit (brown, black, yellow) and confirm it with the meter: 95 to 105 kΩ.

  3. Place it from a free hole in the GAIN strip to a free GND hole. Meter continuity from its far leg to A1's GND pin: steady beep. Meter from the GAIN strip to VIN: no beep.

  4. Plug USB in and ask a question. It should be clearly louder. Too loud for the desk? Remove the resistor to go back to 9 dB, or use the jumper for 12 dB.

1:10First cardboard mock-up

A 1:1 cardboard layout answers the questions a drawing hides: does the USB plug reach, can a finger reach the button, is there a path for sound out and sound in. It cannot check radio; it only checks space.

  1. Cut a paper outline for each measured part, 1:1, and label it.

  2. Arrange them on cardboard until the rules above hold. Trace the case outline around them with 2 to 3 mm of margin.

  3. Hold the real board next to it with the USB cable plugged in, and check the cable really bends that way.

  4. Photograph the layout from above with a ruler in the frame.
