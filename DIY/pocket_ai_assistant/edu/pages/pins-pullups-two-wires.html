Pins, pull-ups and two wires

How A1 talks to its OLED screen, in seven small pictures. No formulas. By the end you can say what a pin does, why a pull-up is there, and what travels on SDA and SCL.

1. A pin is a valve

Each GPIO pin can do one of two jobs. As an output, A1 decides the voltage. As an input, A1 just looks at the voltage someone else set.

2. 1 and 0 are just high and low

3. A pull-up is a gentle spring

A pin with nothing connected floats, like a flag in the wind: it can read 1, then 0, then 1. A pull-up is a weak resistor to 3.3 V that holds it at 1 until something stronger pulls it down.

4. I2C is two shared wires

A1 talks to the OLED over just two wires. SDA carries the data. SCL is the clock, a steady beat that says "read now". Each wire is simply the jumper between the two boards.

5. Nobody pushes up. Everyone can pull down.

On SDA and SCL, a chip may only pull the wire down or let go. The pull-up lifts the wire whenever everyone lets go. That way two chips can share a wire and never fight.

6. One message: an address, then data

The ESP32 beats the clock. On every tick, both chips look at SDA and write down one bit. The first 7 bits are an address, like a house number. Only the chip with that number answers, by pulling SDA down for one tick: that is the ACK, "yes, that's me".

Remember just three things

One

A pin either sets a voltage (output) or reads one (input). HIGH is 1, LOW is 0.

Two

A pull-up is a weak spring that holds a pin at 1 until something pulls it down.

Three

I2C is SDA (data) and SCL (beat). Chips only pull down; an address picks who answers.

Check yourself

Your OLED stops working and the log says no ACK. What do you check first?

The two jumpers (SDA to GPIO21, SCL to GPIO20) and the OLED's power pins. No ACK means nobody answered the house number, which is almost always wiring or the address.

Why does GPIO10 read 1 when nobody touches the button?

Its pull-up holds it at 3.3 V. Pressing the button connects it to ground, which beats the weak pull-up, so it reads 0.

Why can't two chips on SDA ever fight?

Neither can push the wire up. They can only pull it down or let go, and pulling down always wins, so there is never one pushing up while another pulls down.
