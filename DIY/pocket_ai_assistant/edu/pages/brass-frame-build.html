Before you start

  1. Print the template edu/pages/frame-template-letter.pdf on cardstock: Letter, Actual size, black and white, one sheet at a time. Check its ruler measures exactly 100 mm.

  2. Buy baking soda and cotton swabs. You need them right after soldering to neutralize the acid flux.

  3. Box up every electronic part, including the spares and the loose prototype parts, and close the lid. The acid flux and brass chips must never reach them.

Every cut, in order

You saw at three moments: two quick cuts on each tube stick before marking, one cut on each loop after bending, and the rod pieces after the loops are closed. Nothing is cut to its final loop length while the tube is still straight.

0:10Fit a blade and learn the saw

Tool: SE 3-IN-1 JEWELER'S SAW with its blades and V-slot bench pin · B06XPSLS6N · never flush cutters: they crush the 0.225 mm wall

  1. Pick a fine blade from the 144-blade pack: size 2/0 or 3/0, printed on each bundle. Finer blades cut thin tube cleanly; coarse ones catch and snap.

  2. Clamp the bench pin to the table edge, V-slot facing you, flat side up.

  3. Fit the blade as drawn below: top end first, then flex the frame, then the bottom end.

  1. Safety glasses on. Sit so the bench pin is in front of your chest and you look straight down at the V.

  2. Place the stick across the V with your mark centred over the gap. One finger presses it down on each side of the V.

  3. Hold the saw upright, blade at 90° to the stick, resting on the mark.

  4. Start the cut with two or three light upward strokes. They make a small notch so the blade does not skate off the mark.

  5. Saw with long, light strokes, using most of the blade's length. It cuts on the down stroke; just guide it on the way up. If you have to push, the blade is dull or too coarse.

  6. Roll the stick toward you a little every few strokes, so you cut around the tube instead of straight through it. The end stays round and square instead of pinched.

  7. Slow down at the end and support the short piece with a finger so it does not snap off and bend.

  8. Deburr with the 400 grit sandpaper (B07D2YYC11): rub the cut end flat on the sheet in small circles, then twist a scrap of sandpaper inside the tube's mouth. Drag a cotton swab across the end; repeat until it does not snag.

Blades snap while you learn

That is why the pack has 144. When one breaks, fit a new one the same way and carry on. A blade usually snaps from pushing too hard, twisting it in the cut, or a slack fit.

0:20First cuts: square the end, cut to 205 mm

Use: TUBE · 1.5 mm, hollow · K&S #9831 · B005WPAW9M · two sticks, one per loop

  1. Cut 1, square the end. Saw about 1 mm off one end of the stick. Factory ends are often pinched; this end becomes the loop's start, so it must be clean, round and square. Deburr it.

  2. Mark 205 mm from that squared end. Lay the stick on the template's tube marking strip (section 3) with the squared end on the 0 line and mark at the line labelled cut 2 · 205, just past the dashed tail. Your calipers only reach 150 mm, so use the printed strip for this one.

  3. Cut 2, at the 205 mark. This is a forgiving cut: 2 mm of spare is built in, so it is a good one to learn on.

  4. Keep the short piece, about 94 mm. It is your practice piece: saw two or three test cuts on it, and do one test bend on it before you bend the real stick.

  5. Repeat cuts 1 and 2 on the second stick. Label the two long pieces “loop A” and “loop B” with the marker.

0:30Mark the tube

Use: TUBE · 1.5 mm, hollow · K&S #9831 · B005WPAW9M

0:40Bend the corners

Use: TUBE · 1.5 mm, hollow · the two sticks you marked

Tool: ROUND-NOSE PLIERS from the WORKPRO 3-pack · B0B8QBVXXR · the pair whose two tips are both smooth, round cones

  1. Bend 1 to bend 4 in order, all in the same direction, so the tube closes into a rectangle.

  2. After bend 4, check the whole loop lies flat on the table. Twist gently by hand to correct it.

  3. Repeat for the second loop. Lay the two loops on top of each other: they should match within about 0.5 mm.

1:10Close each loop

Use: the bent TUBE loops, plus ROD · 1.0 mm, solid · K&S #9861 · B005WPB7YG for the two 6 mm pins (cut later, in the posts step, or now if you prefer)

  1. Lay the loop flat. After bend 4 the tail lies beside the start end.

  2. Mark the tail exactly level with the start end's face. Check it is near your 192.8 mark; within 1–2 mm is normal.

  3. Spring the tail out a few millimetres so only the tail sits across the bench pin's V, with the rest of the loop flat on the pin. Saw at the mark, rolling the tail as before.

  4. Square and deburr the new end, then bring the two ends together: they should meet with no overlap and the loop should lie flat. A gap up to about 0.3 mm is fine; the pin and solder fill it. A bigger gap: close it by tightening the nearest corner slightly.

  5. Repeat for the second loop.

Fitting the pin, step by step

Use: the 2 short ROD pins (6 mm, solid 1.0 mm) · a fine marker · the second pair of WORKPRO pliers with flat, pointed tips (chain-nose) to hold the pin

If the pin will not go in

Twist a small scrap of 400 grit sandpaper, or the finest round needle file, inside the tube's end to clear the burr, and try again. If it still will not go, leave the pin out and solder the two ends straight together, holding them in line in the helping hands.

1:25Cut and fit the four posts

Use: ROD · 1.0 mm, solid · K&S #9861 · B005WPB7YG. Not the tube.

  1. Square the rod's end with a light cut, as on the tube.

  2. Mark all six pieces at once on the template's rod marking strip (section 4): squared end on 0, then a line at each of cuts 4 to 9. Posts are spaced 33.5 mm and pins 6.5 mm; the extra 0.5 mm is what the saw removes.

  3. Saw on each line on the bench pin, starting from the end. The rod is solid, so it takes a few more strokes than the tube; no need to roll it.

  4. The two pins come last (cuts 8 and 9). Work over a sheet of white paper: short pieces fly off and are hard to find.

  5. File each post to fit at its own corner, flat end, a little at a time, and number post and corner.

After the frame

One decision before any electronics go in

The project rules still say the metal case stays off this USB device. Before mounting, Claude updates them with your OK: the frame is isolated and carries no current, it never touches ground, USB or either speaker wire, it is lined with Kapton wherever a part could touch it, and A1's antenna end stays as clear of brass as the layout allows.
