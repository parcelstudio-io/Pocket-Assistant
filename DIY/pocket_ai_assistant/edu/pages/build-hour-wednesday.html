0:20 · Does USB reach the 5V pin directly?

Done, 22 September

You measured it on A1: 0.001 V and 0.002 V, beeping loudly in both directions, so the 5V pin is wired straight to USB VBUS, with no diode. On Sunday the amplifier gets the full USB voltage. It also means anything touching the 5V pin is touching the computer's USB supply, so no other source ever goes there while USB is in. Record it as VBUS-01.

On Sunday the amplifier takes its power from A1's 5V pin. SuperMini clones differ: some wire the USB supply straight to that pin, others put a diode in between. A diode lowers the amplifier's supply by a few tenths of a volt and stops the pin from feeding power back into USB. The meter's diode mode answers which one A1 has, with nothing powered.

A · Set up the cable

Your USB cable has two different ends. The small oval USB-C end stays plugged into A1. The big rectangular USB-A end is the one that normally goes into the computer: today it stays out, lying on the bench, so nothing is powered.

  1. Unplug the big USB-A end from the computer. Leave the small USB-C end in A1. Tape the USB-A plug to the bench with a strip of Kapton so it cannot roll, with the open side of the plug facing you.

B · Find which outer contact is VBUS

Look into the open end of the USB-A plug. Inside the metal shell is a plastic tongue, and on it are four flat gold contacts. The two in the middle carry data and don't matter today. The two at the outer edges carry power: one is VBUS, the 5 V supply, and the other is GND. Which side is which depends on how you hold the plug, so the meter decides instead of a picture.

The trick: inside the cable, the GND contact is joined by a wire straight to A1's GND. So with the meter in continuity mode, the GND contact beeps against A1's GND and the VBUS contact does not.

  1. Turn the meter's dial to continuity, the setting with the sound-wave symbol. Touch the two probe tips together: it should beep. Push a spare jumper into A1's GND rail on the breadboard and clip or hold the black probe on its free end.

  2. Touch the red probe tip to one outer contact inside the plug, pressing it flat against the gold. Don't let the tip touch two contacts at once. Listen: a steady tone means that contact is GND.

  3. Move the red tip to the other outer contact. No steady tone, or one short chirp that stops, means that contact is VBUS. Put a small Kapton flag on the plug's shell on the VBUS side so you don't have to find it again.

C · Measure VBUS to the 5V pin, both ways

Now the question itself: is VBUS wired straight to A1's 5V pin, or through a diode? A diode lets current through one way only, so you read once each way and compare.

  1. Turn the dial to diode mode, the diode symbol, a triangle with a bar. Push a spare jumper into the same breadboard strip as A1's pin labelled 5V on its silkscreen.

  2. Reading 1: red probe on the flagged VBUS contact, black probe on that jumper. Write down the number, or OL if it shows that.

  3. Reading 2: swap them, red on the jumper and black on VBUS. Write it down.

  4. Find your pair of readings in the table below, and photograph the meter with A1 in the frame.
