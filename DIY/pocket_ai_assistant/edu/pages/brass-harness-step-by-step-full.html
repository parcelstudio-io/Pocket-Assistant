Brass harness, step by step

20 rods, 2 resistors, 2 capacitors, 5 modules, one 57 × 39 mm card. Every rod below was placed in three dimensions against your 28 September part sizes and checked by computer: no two different signals come within 1 mm of each other without a sleeve, nothing comes within 2 mm of the frame, and nothing sits over the antenna. Each rod gets its own drawing, its cut length, its bends and its meter check. Tick as you go; the page remembers in this browser.

0 of 0 steps done

The minimum

Twelve moves, nothing optional in them. Everything further down the page is detail: open it when a move confuses you, otherwise ignore it.

  1. Trim every module to 2 mm stubs, sand them bright.
  2. Floor card: print the template at Actual size, glue it to cardstock, cut 57 × 39. Tape the five parts onto their outlines with any tape. Two pieces of Kapton: over the USB socket's roof, and a strip on the amp between its stubs and its chip. Mic chip-side down, hole up. That is all the tape that matters.
  3. Two rails, bare, on their lines: 3V3 at x = 24, GND at x = 27. A bit of tape at each end.
  4. Six channel rods: 5 V, GND (plus its 3 mm pin to the rail), the 3V3 tap, DIN, BCLK, WS. One sleeve: on the 5 V rod. Then the meter: GND rail to 3V3 rail silent, each rod only to its own ends.
  5. Four fly-overs: mic SD, amp SD, SCL, SDA. One sleeve: on SCL. Then the meter again.
  6. Screen on a 12.5 mm block: solder SCL and SDA to it, add its two power taps, pull the block.
  7. Button on the socket's Kapton, two rods.
  8. Mic: GND tap, VDD tap, L/R link.
  9. Two resistors, measured first (GAIN and the mic-SD pull-down were dropped 2026-10-14).
  10. Two capacitors (sleeve the 220 µF's two leads, they run side by side) and the speaker leads into the block.
  11. Sweep, then power: GND rail to 3V3 rail, to 5 V, to OUT+ and OUT−, all silent. USB in: eyes, button, mic, then the speaker build, two cold starts.
  12. Frame: Kapton on the inside of the tubes, lower the card in, six dots of glue.

The three rules that are not optional: USB unplugged whenever the iron is on · OUT+ and OUT− never touch GND, a rail or the frame · the frame touches no rod and no rail. Everything else on this page is there to save you a redo, not to keep you safe.

The plan: two sessions

Day 1 — moves 1 to 5, about 4½ hours. Stubs, floor card, rails, the six channel rods, the four fly-overs. You stop with every rod in the channel metering clean and nothing powered: a safe place to leave it. On the bench before you start: the five modules, the 1.0 mm rod, flush cutters, chain-nose pliers, 400 grit, calipers, cardstock and the printed floor template, Kapton, Chip Quik pen, 63/37 solder, the electronics tip, heat-shrink (thinnest size) and the heat gun, the meter, helping hands, a white sheet of paper. The brass-frame kit and the Harris flux stay in their box. USB stays unplugged all day.

Day 2 — moves 6 to 12, about 4 hours. Screen, button, mic taps, the small parts, the tests, the frame. Add the resistors (measured), both capacitors, the speaker, the hot glue gun, and the frame with Kapton on its tubes.

At the end of Day 1, send two photos (from above and from the front) and the result of the meter sweep: GND rail to 3V3 rail, and each of the ten rods to both rails. I check them against the drawings before Day 2 starts.

Words on this page
  • Channel: the empty 8.5 mm strip between A1 and the amp, front to back (x = 21.5 to 30 on the map). The two rails lie in it; the straight rods cross it; the mic sits at its back end.
  • Stub: a header pin clipped to 2 mm. Every joint is a rod soldered beside a stub.
  • Rail: one of the two long bare rods on the card, GND and 3V3. A tap is a short rod from a stub down onto a rail.
  • Fly-over: a rod that leaves stub height and crosses above other rods, at 5.5, 7.5 or 9.5 mm.
  • x, y, height: millimetres from the left wall, from the front wall, and up from the card.
What is different from the earlier pages
  • The mic lies flat, pins up, hole up, at the back of the channel. Its six stubs then stand like a tiny breadboard, and its three power taps are 5–10 mm long instead of 30.
  • The screen floats 12.5 mm up on its four rods, header edge at the front, and overhangs the channel. The I²C rods reach its stubs without crossing the amp.
  • The button sits on the USB socket (two layers of Kapton under it), 5 mm from GPIO5 and 9 mm from GND. No rod runs along the left wall.
  • The 220 µF lies in the right-hand strip, leads along the floor in heat-shrink. On the amp there is no room for it under the screen.
  • Rails sit on the card, not 2 mm up, so the straight rods cross 1.5 mm above them. The old sketch had them touching.
  • A1 and the amp both moved back 1 mm so the rails can turn up at the front; A1 sits on one layer of cardstock so the USB plug body clears the front tube.

How each module is prepared

  • A1 (Meshnology ESP32-C3 SuperMini): pins clipped to 2 mm stubs on both rows, USB at the front, on one layer of cardstock. Two layers of Kapton on the USB socket's shell; the button sits on that.
  • Amp (MAX98357A amplifier): stubs, pin row facing the channel, VIN at the front. A strip of Kapton on the board between the stubs and the chip, where R1, R2 and the 100 nF lie.
  • Mic (AITRIP INMP441): the round Ø13 board. Stubs, then turned over: chip side down on a Kapton pad, stubs and hole up. Turn it like a dial until the SD-VDD-GND row faces the channel; the two rows are 7.6 mm apart, which the calipers can confirm.
  • Screen (Hosyond SSD1306 OLED): stubs pointing down from the back, glass up, header edge at the front. It is held 12.5 mm up by a block while its four rods are soldered, then the block comes out.
  • Button (QTEATAK tactile buttons): legs clipped to 1 mm, sticking out sideways. Meter the two legs on each side first: each side is one contact.
  • Speaker (Same Sky CES-20134-088PM): untouched. Its own leads go into the terminal block last.
