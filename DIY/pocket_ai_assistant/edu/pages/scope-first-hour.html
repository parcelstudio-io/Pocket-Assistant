First hour with your oscilloscope

A multimeter tells you one number. An oscilloscope draws the voltage as a picture over time, so you can see clocks tick, pulses switch, and rails dip. This guide sets up a Rigol DHO802 or DHO804, compensates the probes, explains the screen, and ends with your first real capture on A1.

Your model

Your purchase record lists the DHO802: 2 channels, 70 MHz, 12-bit. The DHO804 is the same scope with 4 channels. Menus and controls are the same; where this page says CH1 and CH2, a DHO804 also has CH3 and CH4. Menu names below are the usual DHO800 wording; if yours reads slightly differently, look for the same word in the touch menu.

1. What is in the box

2. Set it up

  1. Place it on a clear, flat spot beside the bench, away from the soldering station, with its vents unblocked.

  2. Power it from the supplied USB-C adapter and press the power button. It takes about half a minute to boot.

  3. Let it warm up about 20 to 30 minutes before any measurement you want to trust.

  4. Run self-calibration once, with nothing connected to the inputs: open the menu, find Utility → System → Self-Cal, and start it. It takes several minutes and fine-tunes the scope to its current temperature.

3. The probe, and the one rule

Use 10X

Set the probe switch to 10X and the channel's probe setting to 10X. It loads your circuit ten times less and keeps the full bandwidth. If the two settings disagree, every reading is 10 times wrong.

Ground clip = circuit ground

The ground clip is usually tied to earth through the scope. Clip it only to your circuit's GND. Never to the speaker's OUT+ or OUT−, and never to anything that is not ground.

4. Compensate each probe

A 10X probe has a tiny adjustable capacitor inside. Until it is matched to the scope's input, fast edges come out rounded or spiky, and every reading is slightly wrong. Matching it takes two minutes per probe, and you only redo it when you move a probe to a different channel.

  1. Connect CH1's probe: twist its BNC onto CH1, set the probe to 10X, and set CH1's probe ratio to 10X in the channel menu.

  2. Hook the tip onto the compensation terminal and clip the ground onto its ground tab. Press AUTO.

  3. Turn the trimmer gently with the small plastic tool, or a small screwdriver, until the square wave has flat tops and square corners.

  4. Repeat for CH2 with the second probe. Keep each probe on its own channel from now on.

5. Read the screen

Vertical knobs

Scale sets volts per square: turn it until the wave fills most of the height. Position slides that channel's trace up or down.

Horizontal knobs

Scale sets time per square: turn it until you see a few cycles. Position slides the picture left or right in time.

The trigger holds the picture still

A signal repeats, and the scope redraws many times a second. The trigger tells it where to start each drawing: "wait until the wave crosses this level going up, then draw". Without it, each redraw starts somewhere different and the picture smears.

6. First real capture: A1's audio clocks

This is the test planned for the scope's arrival (I2S-01): check that A1's two audio clocks run at exactly the right speed. It needs nothing but the two probes.

  1. USB unplugged. Hook CH1 onto GPIO1 and CH2 onto GPIO2, using a short spare jumper in each strip if the pins are hard to reach. Clip both grounds to A1's GND.

  2. Plug USB in and wait for A1 to reach idle. Its microphone keeps both clocks running.

  3. Press AUTO, then add the Frequency measurement for CH1 and CH2 from the measure menu.

  4. Compare: CH1 should read 16.00 kHz and CH2 1.024 MHz, each within about 0.5 %. Save a screenshot to a USB stick or the scope's storage.

Quick reference

If you see…Try…
A flat lineAUTO; check the probe is on the right channel and the channel is turned on
A smeared, sliding waveAdjust the trigger level into the middle of the wave; trigger source = the channel you are watching
Numbers 10 times too big or smallProbe switch and channel probe ratio disagree; set both to 10X
Rounded or spiky square edgesCompensate that probe again (section 4)
Wave too small or clipped off the screenTurn the vertical scale knob
Too many or too few cyclesTurn the horizontal scale knob
