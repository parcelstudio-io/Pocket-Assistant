The cardboard mock-up

The electronics work. The device speaks at volume cap 45. What is left is giving it a body, and every enclosure starts the same way: the real parts, arranged at full size until everything fits, the plug reaches, and sound gets in and out. You own spares of every module, so you can lay them out unpowered and leave the working prototype wired. No soldering, no scope, no risk.

Before you lay them out
  • Keep the spares unpowered. No USB, no battery; they sit on cardboard, so nothing can short.
  • Check for header pins. A spare with pins soldered on sits taller than a final part wired with short leads; one without pins is close to final thickness. Note which you used.
  • The screen window comes from the working A1. An unpowered spare shows no lit area, so switch on the working screen to see where the glow sits, and mark that position on the spare's footprint.
  • Leave wire room. The spares are not wired, so keep 2–3 mm around each one for the wires that will join them.

The paper templates are still in edu/pages/mockup-templates-letter.pdf if a spare is missing. Page 2 of that file has the box nets you will use below. Print at Actual size and check that the ruler measures 100 mm.

0:30Try two arrangements

Lay the spare parts on a sheet of cardboard. There is no single right answer; try both of these and keep the one that is smaller and still obeys every rule in the next section.

0:45The rules every layout must pass

1:00Build the cardboard box

  1. Start from a box net. Page 2 of edu/pages/mockup-templates-letter.pdf has a fold-up box and lid for each arrangement. If your real layout is bigger, trace its outline with 2–3 mm margin instead and cut it twice: floor and lid.

  2. Set the wall height to the tallest spare as it lies, plus 3 mm: about 21 mm if the amplifier keeps its pins, about 12 mm if you plan to trim them. Measure the real stack; for the stacked layout that is the screen on top of A1.

  3. Fold or tape the walls up, set the spares inside, and mark the openings where the parts actually sit: USB, screen window, speaker grille, mic hole, button.

  4. Cut the openings with a craft knife on a cutting mat.

1:20Test fit with the real board

  1. Plug the USB cable into the spare board inside the box through the wall opening: does the plug reach the socket, and can the cable bend away? The spare has no power, so this is safe.

  2. Press a finger through the button opening: could it reach a button there?

  3. Check the window against the working A1's lit screen: hold it under the window and see whether the glow fills the opening.

  4. Photograph the box from above and from each side, with a ruler in the frame, and write down its outside size.
