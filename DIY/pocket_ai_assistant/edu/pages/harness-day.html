Rules for the whole day
  • USB unplugged for every joint and every change. Plug in only to test.
  • Electronics tip and the Chip Quik flux pen only. The brass tip and the Harris acid flux stay in their box.
  • The mic's hole sees no flux, no heat gun, no sanding dust. Cover it with a scrap of Kapton until the last test.
  • The speaker's leads are never cut or stripped. They go into the amp's terminal block.
  • Read every label on the board itself before soldering to it, especially the screen's VCC and GND.
  • Anything you can't explain: stop, photograph it, ask.

0:15Prove the firmware before trimming anything

Thirty minutes on the breadboard with jumpers, pins still on. This catches a screen, Wi-Fi or link problem while it is trivial to fix. Nothing here is cut.

0:45Trim the pins to stubs

Use: chain-nose pliers · flush cutters · 400 grit · over a white sheet of paper

Irreversible
  • After this the modules no longer fit a breadboard. If you are not sure, stop here and do the harness another day.

1:45The rails

Use: ROD 1.0 mm · a strip of cardstock as the jig

2:15The rods, one at a time

Same four moves every time: bend to fit, sleeve any crossing on the lower rod first, hold with the helping hands, flux and solder each end for 1–2 seconds. Then the meter. A rod that fails the check comes off now, while it is the only new thing.

4:15The small parts

Each lead is its own rod: measure, bend with the pliers next to the body (never at the body), cut 3 mm past the joint, solder, sleeve if it passes within 2 mm of anything.

5:00Test it, one module at a time

Server running. Plug USB in only for each test; unplug before touching anything.
