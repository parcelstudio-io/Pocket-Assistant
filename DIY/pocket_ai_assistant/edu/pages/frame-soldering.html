Safety, for the whole session
  • Glasses on, and air moving away from your face: a fan or an open window. Flux fumes sting.
  • All electronics boxed and closed, away from the bench. Acid flux must never reach them.
  • Brass stays hot for a minute after each joint, and heat travels along the whole loop. Move hot parts with pliers.
  • Flux on skin: rinse with water. Keep the bottle closed between joints.
  • Iron back in its stand every time you let go of it.

0:00Set up the iron

  1. Fit the brass-only tip, a 2–3 mm chisel, with the iron cold. Keep this tip for brass from now on: acid flux wears it out, and you want a clean tip for the electronics later.

  2. Set 370–400 °C (700–750 °F). Brass soaks up much more heat than a circuit board, so it needs more than electronics work.

  3. Tin the tip: once it is hot, melt a little solder onto it and wipe it on the damp sponge. It should look silver and shiny.

  4. Lay out the mat, toothpicks, solder, the flux bottle (closed), the sandpaper and the folded-card square within reach.

How every joint is made

Stage 1 · joints 1–20:10The two loop seams

Use: the two TUBE loops and the two 6 mm ROD pins · K&S #9831 · B005WPAW9M

Pins in first, before any heat
  1. Draw a marker line around the middle of each pin, 3 mm from each end.
  2. Spring the loop open a few millimetres at its seam. The loop is springy; it closes again on its own.
  3. Push a pin into one tube end up to the line, using the flat, pointed (chain-nose) pliers.
  4. Aim the other end straight at the pin and let it slide on until the two ends touch.
  5. Check: no gap, no kink at the seam, loop flat. Then solder that seam.

The step-by-step drawings are on the Brass Frame Build page, under “Fitting the pin, step by step”. If a pin will not go in, clear the tube's end with a twist of sandpaper; if it still will not, solder the ends without it.

  1. Clean about 3 mm either side of the seam with 400 grit until shiny. Wipe off the dust.

  2. Lay the loop flat on the mat, seam toward you. Clip the far side only if it moves.

  3. Flux: one small drop on the seam with a toothpick. Close the bottle.

  4. Heat: fresh solder blob on the tip, then the tip's flat face onto the seam, touching both tube ends. Hold 2–3 seconds.

  5. Feed: touch the solder wire to the seam on the side away from the iron. About 1–2 mm of wire melts and runs into the seam.

  6. Solder away first, then the iron. Do not move the loop for 5 seconds.

  7. Check: a thin, shiny line fills the seam, and the loop is still flat and straight. Then do the second loop.

An easier way to hold the cube

Holding a post upright between two loops with one hand while the other holds the iron is the hard part, and it is avoidable. Three habits turn the cube into a calm job.

  1. Make the block. Cut cardstock to 57 × 39 mm and glue sheets until the stack is the inside height (or use a scrap of wood). Cut 3 mm off each corner and wrap the corners in Kapton.

  2. Tape the bottom loop flat to the mat, set the block inside it, stand the posts in the corners with a small loop of Kapton holding each one to the block, and rest the top loop on the block.

  3. Tack all four bottoms, then all four tops. One second each, a dot of solder. Check square with the folded card after every tack.

  4. Finish the bottoms (2–3 s, full fillet) with the block still in. Let it cool.

  5. Lift the block out, flip the frame so the top loop lies on the mat, and finish the four top joints with the posts pressing down under their own weight.

Stage 2 · joints 3–60:25Posts onto the bottom loop

Use: the four ROD posts, 32 mm, solid 1.0 mm · K&S #9861 · B005WPB7YG · plus one soldered loop as the bottom

  1. Choose the bottom loop and lay it flat on the mat, seam toward you. The seam side is now the front.

  2. Clean the middle of each corner curve and both ends of every post with 400 grit.

  3. Post 1, front-left corner (joint 3): clip the post in the helping hands near its top and stand it on the middle of the corner curve, on top of the tube.

  4. Square it with the folded card from the front and the side. Adjust the helping hands until there is no gap.

  5. Solder: drop of flux at the base; tip with blob on the tube beside the post, since the tube is the bigger piece and needs most of the heat; solder on the far side of the post. Hold still 5 seconds.

  6. Re-check it is upright. If it leans: a touch of flux, heat the joint until it is shiny again, nudge the post straight with the clip, and hold still while it sets.

  7. Then the diagonal opposite: back-right (joint 4), then front-right (joint 5), then back-left (joint 6), squaring and checking each one.

  8. Check all four: each post top should be the same height, about 33.5 mm above the mat (1.5 mm tube plus the 32 mm post).

Stage 3 · joints 7–100:50The top loop

Use: the second soldered TUBE loop · K&S #9831 · B005WPAW9M

  1. Clean the middle of each corner curve on the top loop, and the four post tops.

  2. Set the top loop on the post tops with its seam on the same side as the bottom seam.

  3. Dry-fit check: every post top touches the loop at the middle of its corner; each corner sits straight above the one below; the calipers read 35 mm outside at each corner.

  4. Hold it: the loop usually stays put by its own weight. If it slides, clip it at the middle of a long side.

  5. Solder front-left (joint 7): flux, tip with blob on the loop's tube beside the post, solder on the far side, hold still.

  6. Re-check the height and squareness, then back-right (8), front-right (9) and back-left (10).

  7. Work quickly. Heat runs down the post toward its bottom joint. Two or three seconds per joint is plenty; if a bottom joint goes shiny, stop and hold still until it sets.

1:20Wash straight away

No baking soda yet? Scrub every joint in hot water with a drop of dish soap, rinse two or three times, then dry with the alcohol. Do the baking-soda soak when you have it, before paint or any electronics. Never leave flux on overnight.

After the last joint

  • Tip care: wipe it, re-tin it, and switch the iron off.
  • Leftover solder bumps get filed flush with fine sandpaper after the wash.
