Build day: every connection, and why

One sheet for the whole device. The brass harness first, on USB, on its jig card; then the PowerBoost, the switch and the cell underneath it. Every line says what joins what and the one reason it is done that way. Cut lengths, bends and per-rod drawings are on the step-by-step page; the battery details on the power page.

Four rules for the day
  • USB unplugged whenever the iron is on. A powered board and a grounded iron tip are a short waiting to happen.
  • OUT+ and OUT− go to the speaker only. Both are switched outputs that sit at 5 V half the time; tied to ground, the amp dies in milliseconds.
  • The frame carries nothing. 2 mm of air or Kapton between any conductor and the brass; a grounded cage also detunes the antenna.
  • One source at a time. A1's 5V pin is its USB VBUS with no diode. PowerBoost on means A1's USB stays empty; flashing means PowerBoost off.

The two pictures to keep beside you

1 · The two rails

A rail is one bare rod that many parts share, so nine power and ground connections become nine short taps instead of nine rods back to A1. Every ground in the device is then the same piece of metal, which is what makes every signal read correctly: a 1 or a 0 is a voltage measured against ground, and two grounds that differ by a few tenths of a volt turn 1s into 0s.

4 · The battery storey

A lithium cell gives 3.7 V falling to 3.0 V. Fed straight to A1 the amp is a whisper and A1's regulator browns out with half the cell still full. The PowerBoost does three jobs the harness cannot: boost the cell to a steady 5.2 V so the harness sees exactly what USB gave it, charge the cell correctly (1 A until 4.2 V, then hold 4.2 V until the current tapers), and share the load so the device can run while charging. The harness does not change; its two power entry points just get a new supplier.

Why the 1200 mAh and not the 500. The device peaks near 0.5 A at 5 V; through the boost that is about 0.75 A from the cell, 1.5C for a 500 mAh cell and over its rating, 0.6C for the 1200 and comfortable. Charging at 1 A is normal for the 1200, abusive for the 500. Why no solder ever touches the cell. It is a foil envelope; heat the tab and the separator inside can melt. It goes in last and comes out first.

5 · The order of the day

  1. Stubs. Clip A1's and the amp's pins flush under their header strips; the short ends on top are the stubs. Screen and mic pins to 2 mm, button legs to 1 mm. Sand every stub bright. Why: a rod lies against a short stub; a long pin would push it off the card.
  2. Jig card. Print the floor template at Actual size, glue to cardstock. Kapton on the USB socket roof (two layers) and a strip on the amp beside its stubs. Tape the modules on their outlines the way up the step page says; shim the amp's pin-free edge to 2.5 mm. Why: everything is positioned on the card and soldered in place; the card comes off at the end.
  3. Rails. 3V3 at x = 24, GND at x = 27, taped. Meter between them: silent.
  4. Six channel rods. 5 V rod, GND rod, GND drop pin, 3V3 tap, DIN rod, then the BCLK and WS rods with their amp taps. Flux, 1–2 s of iron per joint, 320 °C. Why this order: low rods first; everything later crosses above them.
  5. Four fly-overs. Mic SD, amp SD, button rod, and the BCLK and WS continuations to the mic, up to 10.5 mm. Heat-shrink wherever one crosses another under 1 mm.
  6. Screen on its four rods (SCL, SDA, VCC tap, GND tap), floating 17.5 mm up. Button on the socket's Kapton. Mic chip-side down, its three taps and the L/R link.
  7. R1, R3, C1, C2, speaker. Stripe of the 220 µF to GND. Speaker leads to OUT+ and OUT− only.
  8. Power up on USB. Meter sweep first: GND rail to 3V3 rail, to the 5 V rod, to OUT+, to OUT−, all silent. USB in; eyes open, got ip, hello accepted in the server log. Hold the button, ask Pip its name. Then the amplifier build at cap 45 and Pip answers out loud. Unplug, wait ten seconds, plug in, ask once more.
  9. Off the card. Cut the tape and lift the harness as one rigid piece. Kapton inside the frame. Meter frame to every rail and to OUT±: silent.
  10. Bench-test the PowerBoost alone before it meets the harness: cell in (red to +), switch on EN, meter 5.1–5.3 V at its output, then one charge watching the LEDs (yellow charging, green full). The eight steps are on the real-test page.
  11. Assemble the storeys. Shelf with its two wire holes; PowerBoost on it, USB port to the wall; P1–P4 soldered at the PowerBoost end first and led up through the holes; harness onto the shelf; P1 to A1's 5V stub, P2 to A1's GND stub; Kapton over A1's USB; cradle and cell at the bottom, JST in last.
  12. Final checks. Meter frame to everything: silent. Switch on: the same tests as step 8, now with no cable. Charge once through the PowerBoost's port with the device on.
Never
  • OUT+ or OUT− to GND, a rail, the frame, or a scope ground clip.
  • Anything but A1's 3V3 pin feeding the screen or the mic.
  • A1's USB and the PowerBoost output at the same time.
  • Solder, brass, or an iron within 10 cm of the cell. The 500 mAh cell at all.
  • Harris acid flux on this bench: it is for the empty frame only, washed and neutralised before any module arrives.
