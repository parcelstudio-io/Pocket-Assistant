Brass wiring

No insulated wire. Every connection is a bare 1 mm brass rod, soldered point to point like a circuit sculpture, and the device gets 40 % thinner because the header pins go. The electronics do not change: same pins, same resistors, same tests.

Superseded for the build, 10 October

This page is the design idea. The placement below was checked in 3D afterwards and three things changed: the mic lies flat with its pins up, the screen floats on its four rods, and the 220 µF moves to the right-hand strip. Build from Brass Harness, Step by Step, which has every rod with its own drawing.

About twenty pieces of rod, 300 mm in all. Cut each one 10 mm long and trim after bending. The small parts stay the same as on the breadboard: the mic's 10 kΩ (SCK to 3V3 rail) and 100 kΩ (SD to GND rail), the amp's SD pull-down and 100 kΩ GAIN resistor to the GND rail, and the 220 µF + 100 nF across VIN and GND at the amp. Their own leads are the "rods" for those.

How compact it gets

Change to Friday's frame

Keep the two loops you bent: the footprint stays 57 × 39 mm. Cut four new posts at about 18 mm instead of 32 (measure your real stack first). The frame becomes 60 × 42 × 21 mm. The four 33 mm posts you already cut become spare rod.

The four joints

Use: ROD 1.0 mm · K&S #9861 · B005WPB7YG · electronics tip · Chip Quik flux pen · 63/37 solder · USB unplugged

  1. Trim the headers on every module. Grip the black plastic strip with the chain-nose pliers and pull it off the pins, then clip each pin to a 2 mm stub with the flush cutters. Do not desolder anything.

  2. Make the two rails. Cut two 24 mm rods, sand them shiny along their whole length, and push them through a strip of cardstock 3 mm apart: the card is the jig that holds them straight and 2 mm off the floor.

  3. Place the modules on the 57 × 39 card in the drawn positions, stubs facing the channel. Tape them down with Kapton.

  4. Rod by rod, in the build order on the drawing. Bend first, hold with the helping hands, flux, one small joint each end, 1–2 seconds of iron per joint.

  5. After every rod, meter check: the new rod beeps only to its own two ends; the GND rail never beeps to the 3V3 rail or to the 5 V rod.

  6. Sleeve every crossing as you go, on the lower rod, before the upper rod is soldered.

  7. Resistors and capacitors: trim their leads. They come 25–30 mm long; each lead is just the rod that reaches its two points. Measure the resistor with the meter first, hold the lead with the chain-nose pliers next to the body and bend beyond the pliers (never at the body), shape each lead to its stub or rail with a small hook, cut 3 mm past the joint with the flush cutters, solder, and sleeve any lead within 2 mm of another rod. The 6 mm body cannot shrink, so a resistor needs about 10 mm between its two points.

  8. Test per module, in the same order and with the same tests as the wiring plan: button, screen, mic, amp, speaker, then two cold starts.

Rules that do not change

  • The frame carries nothing. The video solders its grounds to the cage; this build does not. Rails and rods stay at least 2 mm from the frame, with Kapton on the frame wherever they come close. A rail touching the frame makes every accidental contact a short, and a grounded frame sits as a ground plane beside A1's already-weak antenna.
  • Neither speaker output touches GND, the GND rail or the frame.
  • 5 V goes only to the amp. The screen and mic get 3.3 V from the 3V3 rail.
  • Mic port: no flux, heat gun or sanding dust near it.
  • USB unplugged for every joint; plug in only to test.
