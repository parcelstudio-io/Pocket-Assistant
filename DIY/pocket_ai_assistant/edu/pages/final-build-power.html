Pip on a battery: the final wiring

The harness does not change. Battery power is one extra board that owns the 5 V, one cell, one switch and one charge port, feeding the same two points the USB socket fed: A1's 5V stub and, through the 5 V rod, the amp's VIN. Every other rod is the one on the harness page and the net list.

Four rules that replace "USB only"
  • One source at a time. A1's own USB port is blocked in this build. To flash, switch the PowerBoost off first, then plug A1 in; unplug before switching back on.
  • Only the PowerBoost touches the cell. Never a wire soldered to it, never the brass, never the iron within 10 cm of it. It plugs in by its JST and unplugs the same way.
  • Only the 1200 mAh cell. The 500 mAh is rated 0.5 A and this device peaks above that; the PowerBoost also charges at 1 A, which is right for 1200 and too much for 500.
  • Everything from the harness page still holds: OUT+/OUT− never to GND or the frame; the frame carries nothing; the amp's cap stays at 45.

Why each pin

  • 5V to A1's 5V stub. That stub is where USB power used to enter, so every rod downstream sees the same 5 V it always did.
  • GND to A1's GND stub, and nowhere else. One ground meeting point means no loop for the amp's current to run through the signal rods.
  • EN through the switch to GND. EN is a logic input that draws microamps, so the small slide switch controls the whole load without carrying its current.
  • JST, not wires, to the cell. The cell's lead is factory-terminated and keyed; the board's charger and protection are the only things that ever touch it.
  • LBO to GPIO6. An open-drain flag that goes low at 3.2 V; a free GPIO reads it so the face can warn before the cell cuts out.
  • A1 USB-C blocked. On A1 the 5V stub is the USB VBUS node with no diode, so a cable there would fight the PowerBoost's output.

How it charges

  • Plug any USB charger into the PowerBoost's port. Yellow LED: charging. Green: full. About 1½ hours from empty at 1 A.
  • The device may stay on while charging. Load sharing runs Pip from the cable and charges the cell with what is left. Switch off and it charges a little faster.
  • Unplug and it runs on the cell: about 5 hours of talking, longer idle (A1 with Wi-Fi 100–300 mA at 5 V, the amp at cap 45 another 100 mA in bursts; 1200 mAh through the boost gives roughly 4 Wh usable).
  • Red LED: low battery. Charge it; the cell's own protection cuts off at 3.0 V before any harm.
  • The #4410 charger you own is not used in the device. It stays a bench charger for a cell on its own.

Where it all goes

What to buy

  • Nothing, since 2026-10-14: the board on the bench is a third-party copy of the Adafruit PowerBoost 1000C sold as "For Powerboost 1000 Charger … 2465 Module" (Amazon #20). Same chips and pin names are claimed, not proven: the real test is what turns the labels on its silkscreen into facts, and its listing warns the charger chip runs hot (~75 °C) while charging.
  • Owned already: the 1200 mAh cell, the slide switch, 26 AWG wire, Kapton, cardstock, the silver wire for the frame.

Order of work

  1. Finish the brass harness on USB and make it work (the harness page). Nothing here starts before that.
  2. Bench-test the PowerBoost alone: cell in, switch, meter 5.2 V on its output, charge it once, watch the LEDs.
  3. Bend and solder the silver frame to the new size.
  4. Cradle, shelf, PowerBoost, two wires up into the harness, switch, Kapton over A1's port.
  5. Meter sweep (frame to everything: silent), switch on, the same tests as move 11.
