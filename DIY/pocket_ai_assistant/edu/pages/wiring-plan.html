How the wiring works

ConnectionWhat it doesWires
PowerUSB brings 5 V into A1. A1 makes 3.3 V for the screen and the microphone, which must never see 5 V. The amplifier is the one part that takes 5 V straight from A1's 5V pin. Every part shares one ground, the return path for all current.5V, 3V3, GND
I²C (screen)Two wires carry everything the screen shows: SCL is the clock, SDA the data. A1 calls the screen by its address, 0x3C or 0x3D.2 + power
I²S (mic and amp)Digital sound. Two clock wires tick 16,000 times a second for each sample: WS marks each sample and BCLK each bit. The mic and the amp share both clocks. Sound comes in from the mic on SD and goes out to the amp on DIN.2 shared + 1 each way
Simple pinsThe button pulls GPIO10 to ground when pressed. GPIO5 turns the amplifier on; its pull-down keeps the amp off while A1 starts up.1 each
SpeakerThe amp pushes and pulls the speaker with two outputs, OUT+ and OUT−. Neither is ground, so they never touch GND or the frame.2, twisted

This is exactly the circuit you already built and tested on the breadboard. The breadboard rails become short soldered wires and two small splices; nothing about the electronics changes.

Colour rule

black = ground, red = power. The only thick red wire is 5 V and goes only to the amplifier. Signal colours repeat between modules, so put a small Kapton flag with the pin name on each end. If your wire kit's colours differ, keep black for ground and red for power and label the rest.

How to solder a wire to a header pin

Use: the electronics tip, not the brass tip · Chip Quik no-clean flux pen, never the Harris acid flux · iron about 330–350 °C (625–660 °F) · USB unplugged

Your modules already have header pins from Step 5. Keep them and solder the wires to the pins: it is far less work than removing the pins, and the stack still fits with about 8 mm to spare.

How it fits inside the brass frame

One change from your mock-up

Your cardboard stack put the screen over A1. Its copper would then sit right over A1's antenna, which already needed its Wi-Fi power capped to be heard. This plan stacks the screen over the amplifier instead and leaves A1's antenna under open air, in the same 57 × 39 mm box. If you would rather keep the screen over A1, tell Claude: the wires stay the same, only some lengths change, and the Wi-Fi check after mounting decides.

Build order

Rules for every wiring session
  • USB unplugged for every solder joint and every change. Plug in only to test.
  • Electronics tip and Chip Quik flux only. The Harris acid flux and the brass tip never touch a module.
  • Mic port: no flux, heat, glue or air near its sound hole.
  • Speaker leads: never cut or strip them; they go into the amp's screw terminal.
  • Read every label on the board itself before soldering, especially the screen's VCC and GND.

  1. Lay out and measure. Parts on the 57 × 39 card in the planned positions; measure and write down every wire length, plus 10 mm.

  2. Cut and prepare all wires: strip 2 mm at each end with the Hakko stripper, twist, tin, and flag each end with its pin name.

  3. Button first (2 wires). Test: plug USB in and press ten times; each press registers, as in fast-track Step 6.

  4. Screen (4 wires). Unplugged meter check: VCC to GND gives no steady tone. Test: the screen lights at start-up and the log shows address 0x3C or 0x3D, as in Step 7.

  5. Microphone (5 wires, the L/R link, two resistors). Test: speech makes the sound level rise compared with quiet, as in Step 8; on the assistant build, it hears a question.

  6. Amplifier (6 wires, pull-down, GAIN resistor, two capacitors), speaker not yet connected. Meter, unplugged: 5V to VIN continuous, VIN to GND no steady tone, OUT+ and OUT− not connected to GND. Powered: SD below 0.08 V before speech, as in PASS 11A.

  7. Speaker on, unplugged, into the amp's terminal. Test: five spoken replies in a row, clear, no reset, nothing warm, at cap 45, as in PASS 11C.

  8. Two cold starts with everything wired. Then the harness is done.

Claude can read the test logs for you: plug A1 in, say which module you just wired, and Claude opens the monitor and checks the result.

Then into the frame

  1. FRAME-01 done, washed and dry (Frame Soldering page).

  2. Project rules updated to allow the isolated frame. Claude makes that change with your OK.

  3. Kapton on every module back and wherever a wire or pin could touch brass.

  4. Place the harness in the frame, nothing glued, and re-run the full test.

  5. Fix the modules with dabs of low-temperature hot glue, away from the mic port and connectors.

  6. Wi-Fi check: compare signal and dropouts inside the frame with the open bench.
