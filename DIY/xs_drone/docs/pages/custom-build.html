XS drone · custom build · no kit

The drone without the kit

Electrically it is the ESP-FLY driver board rebuilt by hand around the same XIAO, so the firmware and every sensor decision carry over. What changes is the shape: a low ladder frame with the battery as the floor pan and the lights at the nose. Assessed on paper 2026-10-28; parts double-checked 2026-11-07; microphone and speaker added 2026-11-09 and approved that night; the science behind them is XS Drone Voice Physics (2026-11-10). The right cell is the Tiny Whoop pack: 1S, 250 to 300 mAh, 30C continuous, JST-PH 2.0 plug, about 7 g, 55 to 60 mm long and 12 mm wide. The length is the point: it becomes the car's floor pan. The owned daier PH 2.0 cable pairs make the pigtail (check the housing mates with the pack's plug). The owned Adafruit #4410 charger fills a pack in about 2.5 h at 100 mA; the XIAO's own USB charger is rated 50 mA in Seeed's wiki, which works but takes five hours. LiHV packs charge safely to 4.2 V on either and give up a little capacity.

As bought (checked 2026-11-07). The four flight packs came with the smaller Micro JST 1.25 plug, not PH 2.0. That plug is rated about 1 A per contact and would carry the 3.7 A hover: the lossiest joint in the power path.

Recommended at build step 1: re-terminate each pack with a PH 2.0 lead from the owned daier pairs (no product link on record), cutting and joining one wire at a time so the cutters never bridge the pack. The packs then plug straight into the USB-C LiPo charger. Meter the polarity at every PH 2.0 housing first: generic PH leads are often wired opposite to Adafruit's.

Keeping the 1.25 plugs also works. The charger then needs an adapter made from one PH 2.0 lead and one of the 1.25 mm leads, and the first hover must run through the real plug.

Fixes found, nothing to buy:

  • Forward ranger on VDD, not VIN. Pololu gives the 8×8 laser ranger 3.3 to 5.5 V on VIN, so the XIAO's 3.3 V would sit on its floor. Its path for a 3.3 V supply is the VDD pin (2.5 to 3.6 V) with VIN left open. Corrected in the pin table, the wiring sheet and under Sensor power.
  • Battery plug. See "As bought" under Battery.
  • Collar former. No 6 mm round bar is on record. Use the owned 6 mm brass tube from the tube set with the 5 mm tube slid inside so it cannot crush, wrapped in one turn of Kapton so the solder cannot grab the brass.
  • LED star pads. The LED star's four pads run out toward its corners. Mark the 14 mm circle before sawing; if it cuts through the pads, stop and re-plan the lamp.
  • Lens glue. Fix the lens by its four legs with the owned low-temperature hot glue, never CA: Carclo warns against solvents and vapour, and CA fumes fog the optic.

The hand-built driver circuit, checked

The ESP-FLY driver board is four low-side motor switches, a battery divider, the MPU6050 and two LEDs. Rebuilt by hand it keeps every GPIO the ESP-Drone configuration already expects.

  • The MOSFET is enough. AO3400A: 30 V, 5.7 A, gate threshold at most 1.45 V, about 35 mΩ with the XIAO's 3.3 V on the gate. A 615 motor runs at 0.7 to 1 A and stalls at 2.3 to 2.8 A; 1 A through 35 mΩ is 35 mW, so the SOT-23 never warms. The kit's SI2300 is the same class of part driven the same way.
  • The diode is enough. Freewheeling current equals the motor current at switch-off, about 1 A peak and much less on average; SS14 is rated 1 A continuous, 30 A surge.
  • Motor polarity. Coreless motors spin either way; CW and CCW labels say which lead is positive for the intended direction (whoop convention: red positive on CW, black positive on CCW). The motor-direction test decides, not the wire colour. Swap the two leads to reverse a motor.
  • The IMU. GY-521 at 0x68 (AD0 pulled low on the board), 3.3 V logic, its own 2.2 kΩ pull-ups, INT brought out. Mount it flat under the floor pan with its X arrow toward the nose and run test C03; if a sign is wrong, rotate the board, do not patch the firmware.
  • Sensor power. The VL53L1X carrier level-shifts SDA and SCL to its VIN (2.8 to 5.5 V), so its VIN takes 3V3, not VBAT. The VL53L7CX carrier wants 3.3 to 5.5 V on VIN, which would leave the XIAO's 3.3 V on its floor, so it takes 3V3 on VDD instead (2.5 to 3.6 V, VIN left open), Pololu's path for a 3.3 V supply; its SDA, SCL and LPn then sit at VDD (corrected 2026-11-07). The GY-521 goes on 3V3 too, so all three share one logic level. That rail then carries the ESP32, camera, three sensors and the mic, about 500 mA at the camera's peak, inside the 700 mA Seeed's wiki allows. The flashlight and the motors run from VBAT and never touch it.
  • The divider. 10 kΩ + 10 kΩ: GPIO2 reads half the cell, 2.1 V full, 1.65 V empty. Same as the kit, so the battery display needs no change.
  • Left out. The kit's green and red LEDs; the flashlight is the status light. The blue LED and buzzer pins are already disabled in the config for the camera.

Mass of the hand-built board: four SOT-23, four SMA diodes, ten resistors, a capacitor, the GY-521 and the wire, about 2.5 g est. against the kit board's 1 g. The frame gives the difference back.

Voice: microphone and speaker, added and approved 2026-11-09

The builder asked for a microphone and a small speaker on the evening of 2026-11-09 ("small ones are fine"). The microphone was already there: the Sense board carries a digital (PDM) microphone wired inside the XIAO on GPIO41 and 42, so it costs no pin and no mass, and the ledger has counted it all along. The speaker is new, and the constraint is pins, not size: the XIAO brings out eleven pins and the drone uses ten, so D10, GPIO9, is the only one free. The owned I²S amplifiers need three pins and are out. The speaker therefore takes an analog path from that one pin, drawn on the wiring sheet above.

  • DAC. The ESP32-S3's sigma-delta modulator (eight channels, any GPIO) runs on GPIO9 at a 1 MHz pulse rate as an 8-bit DAC; a 16 kHz hardware timer updates the density from a ring buffer. Espressif documents exactly this use and asks for a low-pass filter after the pin.
  • Filter and level. GPIO9 → 10 kΩ → node; node → 1.5 kΩ to GND and 22 nF to GND; node → the amplifier's left input. The two resistors divide the 3.3 V swing to 0.43 V peak to peak at full scale, and with the capacitor they roll off above 5.6 kHz: speech in, the megahertz pulses out. The amplifier's 24 dB of gain makes that about 6 V peak to peak across the speaker, 0.6 W into 8 Ω at 3.7 V, which is the speaker's rating; the firmware runs 6 dB under that by default. All three parts come from the owned kits.
  • Amplifier. A PAM8403 class-D board (to buy, a 5-pack; the version without the volume knob; about 18.5 × 21 mm, 1.5 g est. with its header pads bare), fed from VBAT, inside its 2.5 to 5.5 V range, with a 47 to 100 µF reservoir from the owned kit across its power pads. Left channel only: IN R and both right outputs stay open, and OUT L− never meets GND, because the outputs are bridged. The board has no shutdown pad, so a soft pop at power-up is expected. The listing was not re-read (Amazon blocks the agent); the board is the common one.
  • Speaker. One of the two spare Same Sky enclosed speakers from Pip's stock (datasheet): 20 × 16 × 4.9 mm, 21 mm over its two flanges, 2 g typical, 8 Ω, 0.8 W, 91 dB at 10 cm, sealed back, black housing, 60 mm leads. The bare 15 × 11 mm element inside it is 0.5 g but loses the sealed back and would be a DigiKey order; the owned part wins.
  • Where. The speaker lies on the tail of the floor pan behind the cabin, grille up, from −10.5 to −30.5 mm, under the rear prop discs with its top at 10.9 mm, 5 mm under the prop plane; two clear-tape straps hold it with the pack and a loop of 30 AWG through each flange hole ties it to the rails. The amplifier hangs under the pan at the nose ahead of the motion sensor, +11 to +29.5 mm, 0.5 mm short of the front axle, components toward the floor, 1.5 mm above it on the skids. Its leads to the speaker run along the pan's edge under the straps (no sleeves: rule 2026-11-09). The two masses sit about 20 mm either side of the centre and nearly balance.
  • Firmware. Two items join the payload plan's list: a microphone task (I²S PDM in at 16 kHz, streamed up) and a playback task (the ring buffer and the timer). Both speak the WebSocket protocol Pip's character server already serves, over the Wi-Fi link following uses; the desk computer does the listening, thinking and talking.
  • Honest limit. In flight the props are centimetres from both. It can announce while flying; it will not hear you over the motors, so voice commands in flight go through the desk computer's own microphone. Talking both ways is for when it has landed or perches next to you.
  • Cost. About 4.0 g: the speaker 2.0, the amplifier 1.5 est., filter, reservoir and ties 0.5 est. The estimate goes from 33.5 to 37.5 g and thrust to weight from 2.03 to 1.81, under the 2.0 gate but inside the 1.5 floor that test D02 allows (hover command about 74 % instead of 70 %); hover current about 4.4 A, flight about 2.6 min. If the first hover reads under 1.8, the choices are the bare 0.5 g element or the 716 motors already in the plan.
  • Pin note. GPIO9 is also the Sense board's SD-card MOSI line. No card ever goes in that slot on this aircraft; the empty socket leaves the line free.
  • Finish. The speaker's housing is black, one of the family's colours, so it stays as bought; its red and black leads run under the straps along the pan's edge. The amplifier board is blue and simply hides under the hood; nothing is applied to anything (no paint, no skins, no sleeves: builder's rule 2026-11-09).
  • Approved and explained. Approved late on 2026-11-09. Step two, the science and engineering behind every number here, is XS Drone Voice Physics (2026-11-10; source xs_drone/docs/voice-physics.md, generated by pages/gen/voice_page.py, PDF in pages/print/). Two things from it for the firmware: the microphone task asks the I2S driver for the 128× clock ratio (2.05 MHz at 16 kHz; the default 64× gives 1.02 MHz, in a gap between the microphone datasheet's low-power and standard bands), and the playback task keeps the everyday level at ±64, 6 dB under full scale, and mutes the microphone while it plays. The amplifier link is the knob-less listing in the builder's cart (11-09, $5.59, not ordered); C11's quiescent limit is 20 mA, the datasheet's typical being 10 mA at 3.6 V.

The car-like layout

The kit is a square. A car is longer than wide, low, with its lights at the nose. The custom frame is a ladder: two 1.0 mm stainless rails 14 mm apart along the body, two cross rods at the axle positions carrying the motors outboard, a nose bar for the headlights and a tail bar. The battery lies between the rails as the floor pan; the XIAO stack sits on it as the cabin; the GY-521 and the down-looking VL53L1X hang under the pan.

Why 40 × 60 and not smaller: the four 31 mm prop discs must not pass over anything taller than 16 mm. Spacing the motors 60 mm fore and aft moves the front discs clear of the cabin, so the XIAO stack can stand 21 mm tall at the centre. Laterally 40 mm leaves 9 mm between adjacent prop tips. At the nose the discs do pass over the headlight bar, so everything there stays under 15 mm: the flashlight star is 14 mm tall from 3 mm below the rails, the VL53L7CX board 13 mm. The camera is on the Sense board in the cabin, not at the nose; it looks forward over the hood from 21 mm up on the centre line, where no disc reaches.

The two headlights differ in function, and that is the car reference: a lamp on the left, a sensor on the right, both facing forward. If the flashlight must sit on the centre line as first asked, the VL53L7CX moves to the hood in front of the cabin under the camera, and the beam and the depth map still overlap.

Rod: rails 2 × 66 mm, axles 2 × 40 mm, nose bar 31 mm, tail bar 14 mm, four 5 mm skids: 277 mm of 1.0 mm rod, one 300 mm stick, 1.7 g. Four two-turn 1.5 mm collars at the axle ends, 48 mm of rod each, 2.7 g; cut 60 mm blanks to wind. Every joint is a lap or a T on the stainless procedure in the inventory. The battery is a stressed member once strapped: it stops the ladder racking.

Colour and finish

Stainless, white and black, under the family rule of 2026-11-07 (white or ivory as much as possible, black where a part comes black, nothing green, blue or red in sight) and the builder's second rule of 2026-11-09: no painting and nothing applied over a part, no card, no film, no sheet, no sleeve, no custom cover. The order of choice is a part that comes in white, ivory, black or bare metal; the same part bought in a matching or complementary colour; hiding by placement inside the build. So everything is as bought: the frame bare brushed stainless; the props, the controller, the speaker and the regulators black; the pack's white wrap label-down; every bare board under the pan or the hood. One colour shows: the forward ranger's 13 mm green board at the nose, recessed between the rails. The headlight is white light; the controller's gold pads and the camera's amber ribbon are the only warm notes.

Build order, one variable per step

  1. Power first. Packs re-terminated to PH 2.0 (see Battery), then the pigtail, divider, 220 µF, XIAO on the bench supply at 3.7 V with the limit at 0.5 A. The app shows the voltage the meter shows. Then the speaker path on the same supply: a 1 kHz tone from the DAC, 0.43 V peak to peak at the filter node on the scope, clean at the speaker (test C11).
  2. One switch cell on the bench, motor without prop, supply limited to 1.5 A. PWM sweep from the firmware's motor test. The MOSFET stays cold, the motor stops at zero, the diode band points at VBAT. Then the other three.
  3. IMU. GY-521 under the pan, test C03: nose down, right side down, clockwise yaw, each with the right sign. Rotate the board until they are.
  4. Solder the ladder frame, mount everything, put on the finish. Motor-direction test with no props, then props on. The thrust gate is read in the first hover (step 5, test D02). Under 2.0 means 716 motors before anything else.
  5. First hover over a bed, IMU only, lamp off. This is the custom build's baseline, the flight the kit would have given for free. Read the thrust gate here: a full-throttle punch from a low hover, the accelerometer's z log peak in g is the thrust-to-weight ratio; without the log, hovering at 70 % thrust or less passes.
  6. Then as the payload plan says: VL53L1X and height hold, flashlight and beeper, VL53L7CX last, then the voice loop over the Wi-Fi link (test C12).
