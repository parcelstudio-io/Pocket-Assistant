What a 32 g drone can carry

The battery, decided

The owned Nitecore NL169 stays with Cyber Dawn and the Pocket AI. Two independent reasons, either one fatal.

The whole kit, battery included, is 25 g. The NL169 alone would be 80 % of that, and its protection circuit would open in the air. No multirotor small enough to live on a 2 A cell can lift a 20 g cell. The kit LiPo charges from the XIAO's USB-C in two to five hours, or in minutes on a 1S whoop charger.

Both ST rangers wake at 0x29. The VL53L7CX's LPn only disables its I2C, so it is the one to hold while the VL53L1X takes 0x2A through a register. The new address dies at power-off; the firmware repeats this every boot. Sources: Pololu 3415, ST UM2884.

Firmware: four collisions, two drivers

The kit firmware is a vendored ESP-Drone. Its committed sdkconfig carries pins from Espressif's own ESP32-S2 drone board that do not exist on a XIAO, and four of them land on the Sense camera.

Free once the VL53L1X sits on I2C0: boot auto-detect, range into the Kalman estimator, height-hold above 40 cm. Position hold needs the PMW3901 optical-flow sensor on four SPI pins the XIAO does not have free, so it is out. To write:

  1. Camera task. The esp32-camera component with the XIAO Sense pin table, QVGA JPEG at 5 to 10 fps as UDP frames on a second port, or stills on request. Shared with the Pocket AI with Camera port. Seeed's FAQ says the Sense camera is not supported by the current ESP-FLY workflow; this is the work that makes it so.
  2. Multizone driver. ST's VL53L7CX ULD library on the ESP-Drone I2C layer. The chip loads about 90 KB of firmware over I2C at every power-on, about a second. Output: an 8×8 distance grid at 15 Hz. First a telemetry stream to the phone, then a wall guard that clamps forward pitch when the middle rows read under 40 cm.
  3. Flashlight and beeper. LEDC PWM on GPIO43 into the AMC7135 VDD pins. Brightness is duty, a beep is the same pin at 4 kHz, and the duty is capped at 50 % whenever the motors are stopped.
  4. Boot re-addressing. GPIO44 low, init the VL53L1X, set 0x2A, GPIO44 high, init the VL53L7CX. Before auto-detect runs.

Open risk: the Wi-Fi link. Control is a few kB/s of UDP; QVGA JPEG at 8 fps is about 100 kB/s on the same radio. First camera flights use stills on request, with link-loss measured before any stream. The Sense runs hot streaming for minutes; the frame leaves the chip side open to propwash.

From the Seeed wiki. The camera order of 2026-10-27 may have delivered two Sense boards; the second is this drone's brain. The analog alternative, a 2.8 g AKK C1T camera and 25 mW transmitter, is lighter and costs the ESP32 nothing, but its picture goes to goggles, not to the drone, and it has no mic.

Speaker. The owned MAX98357A needs BCLK, LRC and DIN. The header has one spare pin and the two LED pins, exactly the set the forward ranger and the flashlight use. It would also add 3.5 g and 250 mA peaks: 35.5 g, a 1.9 ratio, under the floor. Substitute: a 12 mm piezo (TDK PS1240, DigiKey) across the flashlight LED. It is a few mA of capacitive load; 4 kHz PWM on the flashlight pin beeps it while the LED flickers invisibly. Speech stays with the Pocket AI and the phone.

Flashlight

A real flashlight, not an indicator. A Cree XP-G2 reflowed on a 20 mm aluminium star (Indus Star 1-Up, cool white; two bought 2026-10-28, one plus a spare), behind a Carclo 10412 10 mm narrow-spot TIR lens, driven by two AMC7135 350 mA linear regulators in parallel. It stands vertically at the nose on the centre line, pointing where the camera looks, 10° down.

PWM dutyCurrentPowerLightBeamHeat into the starCost in flight
50 %350 mA avg1.0 Wabout 150 lm23°, about 1 000 cd: 110 lux at 3 m0.65 W+0.35 A, about 0.3 min
100 %700 mA2.0 Wabout 250 lmsame lens, about 1 800 cd1.3 W+0.7 A, about 0.5 min

For scale: a phone torch is 30 to 50 lm in a wide flood; a Nitecore TIP keychain light is 360 lm on turbo. Half power here is three times a phone torch, concentrated into a spot about a door's width at 2.5 m.

  • Emitter. XP-G2 R5 bin: 139 lm minimum at 350 mA, 254 lm at 700 mA, forward voltage 2.8 and 2.9 V, 1.5 A maximum (datasheet). Same footprint and optics as the XP-G, so every 10 mm Carclo lens fits. LEDSupply shows 0 in stock; the Amazon listing is the same LEDdynamics board.
  • Regulator, not resistor. The LED drops 2.8 V while the cell falls from 4.2 to 3.3 V in a flight; a resistor sized for 350 mA at 4.2 V passes 130 mA at 3.3 V. The AMC7135 is a current sink: 350 mA whenever its input is 0.12 V above the LED, which holds to a 3.0 V cell. 2.7 to 6 V, no external parts, the chip inside most 1S flashlights (datasheet). Fit one chip if 150 lm is enough.
  • No MOSFET. GPIO43 drives the AMC7135 VDD pins directly. The chip is off below 2.7 V and VDD draws under 1 mA, so the 3.3 V pin is both enable and PWM input, as the Nanjg 105C flashlight driver does with an ATtiny pin. The kit's 200 Ω series resistor can stay. Fallback: an AO3400A low-side on the chips' GND pins.
  • Heat. 1 W in is 0.65 W of heat, 2 W is 1.3 W. A star sawn to 14 mm rises about 60 °C per watt in still air, far less in prop wash. On the bench with the props stopped, hold 50 % duty; the firmware enforces it. Stainless is not a heatsink (16 W/m·K against aluminium's 200), so the star keeps its own metal: 14 mm, not smaller.
  • Why 14 mm. The star stands between the two front prop discs, whose tips are 6.8 mm apart at the centre line and 20 mm above the arms. A 14 mm disc mounted 2 mm above the arm tops out at 16 mm, 4 mm under the props; the full 20 mm hex would not clear. The lens sits 7 mm proud of the nose, 9 mm below the camera's lens centre: out of the picture.
  • Mass. 14 mm disc 0.7 g, lens 0.4 g, two SOT-89 chips 0.2 g, piezo 0.3 g, wire and solder 0.4 g: 2.0 g est.
  • It is also the camera's light. The 23° spot is the middle third of the OV2640's 66° picture.

The piezo stays wired across the LED: the LED node swings 2.8 V at the PWM rate, and 4 kHz beeps it while the beam flickers invisibly.

Attitude: tilt, yaw and heading

The sensor is already on the kit. The driver board carries an MPU6050, a 6-axis inertial measurement unit: a 3-axis accelerometer and a 3-axis gyroscope in one 4 × 4 mm chip, on I2C0 at 0x68 with its data-ready interrupt on GPIO8. The flight controller cannot work without it; it is what keeps the drone level, and acceptance test C03 checks its axes.

What you wantWhich sensorHow it is got
Tilt: roll and pitchaccelerometer + gyroscopeThe gyro integrates turn rate for a fast estimate; the accelerometer sees where gravity points and trims the drift. ESP-Drone fuses the two several hundred times a second.
Yaw rategyroscope Z axisread directly
Heading: which way is northmagnetometer, not fittedA gyro-only yaw drifts a degree or so a minute, so the drone holds the heading it had at arming: relative, not absolute.
HeightVL53L1X in this planthe MPU6050 has no barometer, and a barometer cannot resolve 10 cm indoors anyway
Pannot a sensorthe camera is fixed to the airframe; a quad pans by yawing

A magnetometer would cost no pin: a QMC5883L or HMC5883L breakout on I2C0 at 0x0D or 0x1E, 0.3 g, and ESP-Drone's sensor file is literally named sensors_mpu6050_hm5883L_ms5611.c. Still not recommended: four brushed motors 26 mm away and a 700 mA lamp wire are magnets and current loops a few centimetres from the sensor, and indoor steel bends the field by tens of degrees. A three-minute indoor flight does not need true north. If wanted later it is a $5 part and a config line.

The 68 gf is the vendor's claim for four 615 motors on 30 mm props, unmeasured here. If the thrust stand says under 2.0, the fix is 716 motors on 40 mm props (2.9 g each, 13 gf at 1.05 A on 32 mm, more on 40 mm), which the kit's SI2300 switches carry. 8520 motors are out: the JST-PH connector and the cell cannot feed them.

Size and layout

The kit is 67 × 67 × 31 mm on about a 52 mm diagonal wheelbase with 30 mm props. The Rev A printed frame was 75 mm for print tolerance; the rod frame does not need that margin, so the stainless build targets the kit's own geometry.

Frame construction

  • Arms. Two 1.0 mm rods, 75 mm each, crossed through the centre. 0.9 g.
  • Decks. A 22 × 26 mm rectangle of 1.0 mm rod soldered on top of the cross for the driver board, on Kapton; a second 8 mm lower cradles the battery. 1.2 g.
  • Motor collars. Two turns of 1.5 mm rod wound on a 6.2 mm former, about 40 mm each, soldered to the arm tips. The 615 motors press in, a drop of CA holds them. 2.2 g, half the frame. The owned 150 mm coupons supply all four.
  • Skids. Four 1.0 mm legs, 4 mm, under the collars.
  • Floating. The frame touches no net. Motor leads and sensor wires keep 2 mm from any rod; Kapton under every board.

Stiffness: a 1.0 mm stainless rod has twice the bending stiffness of the Rev A PLA arm, a 1.5 mm rod ten times. 1.0 mm is enough for a 2 g motor at 26 mm, and the crossed rods brace each other. Not modelled: prop guards and a camera cover. First flights are over a bed.

Build order, one variable per step

  1. Fly the kit stock on its own frame with the plain XIAO, as assembly-plan.md says. Weigh everything. Run the thrust stand. This is the baseline.
  2. Bench the Sense swap on USB, props off: camera stills over Wi-Fi, mic capture, the four config changes, the flight stack still arming and reading the IMU.
  3. VL53L1X on I2C0, bench: auto-detect logs it, height telemetry reads a ruler. Fly height-hold on the kit frame.
  4. Solder the stainless frame, move the electronics, weigh, thrust-stand again. Fly.
  5. Flashlight and beeper. Bench at 50 % duty with the props off, then fly at full. Weigh first.
  6. VL53L7CX and its driver. The 8×8 grid in telemetry first, the wall guard last.

If step 4's thrust ratio is under 2.0, stop and fit 716 motors before adding anything else.
