## The reference

Huy Vector, [DIY Micro RC Spy Tank](https://www.huyvector.org/robots-kinetic/diy-micro-rc-spy-tank) and its [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PszrmrE7JQU), read 2026-11-06, the treads re-read 2026-11-07 from 3:30 to 5:30: a 1 mm copper-wire frame, two DC gear motors, three 16340 cells and two USB-C charging modules, a toy RC receiver board, an analog FPV camera, two 3 W LEDs on one 56 Ω resistor, a switch, and treads of short rods soldered side by side, tied with thread round four grooved aluminium drums that PCBWay machined (3:16 to 3:44). Ours keeps the open wire frame, the soldered treads, the camera, the light, the 16340 and USB-C charging, and becomes a wide little tank with a low turning head.

## The decisions

### A brass spine, the boards on it

No box and no paint. Two brass treads carry a frame of six straight brass tubes, lapped and soldered: two 3 mm rails down the middle (the spine), two rungs under the motors, a short back bar, and a 4 mm axle across the front that the idler drums turn on. No bends, 8 laps. The servo stands on the rails between the axle and the back bar; the head turns on it; the screen, the laser ranger and the flashlight hang on the axle. 114 wide, 90 long, 60 mm tall, about 332 g. You drive it from a phone over Wi-Fi, watch through its camera and talk to it.

### The frame carries the wires and the ground

The rails are tubes with open ends, so the long runs go inside them: BAT+ and the I²C bus in the left rail, the three sound lines and the servo's 5 V in the right. The frame is ground, as in a circuit sculpture: each board's GND is soldered to the nearest tube. Brass takes solder far more easily than Rev J's stainless did.

### Treads you solder, like the reference's

Huy Vector's treads are links of short rods soldered side by side and tied with thread round grooved drums. Ours: each plank is a 3/32 in [brass tube](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B079VL3XD1) with two 1 mm brass rods soldered under it, side by side on the tread face. The tube is 4 mm longer; its ends are the hinge pins. A loop of 24-gauge nickel silver joins each pair of neighbouring pins. 32 planks a track, 6.5 mm apart, 128 loops in all.

### Laser-cut drums, bare

Four drums of two 3/8 in 6061 halves each, cut on [SendCutSend's laser](https://sendcutsend.com/materials/6061-aluminum/) and already ordered: 19.1 mm wide, 16 round seats, 35.0 mm over the teeth. No paint: bare aluminium against the brass. The drive drums are glued on 21 mm brass hubs crimped on the [gearmotors'](https://www.pololu.com/product/1097) flats. The idlers turn on the brass axle itself between two collars, so the two idler screws are gone.

### No paint, no skins: every part in its own colour

Your rule of 2026-11-09. Every part that shows was read off its maker's photos. Black where black exists: the camera board, the screen's board, both laser rangers, the charger, the cell holder and the speaker; the drone's green 8×8 and Rev J's blue charger, green boost and green ranger give way to black ones. What only comes in another colour sits where nothing sees it: the blue motor controller and the purple amplifier hang under the motors, the translucent-blue servo stands under the head and behind the screen, and the switch's blue body stands between the holder and the speaker, behind the screen. The cell's yellow print faces down. Lights stay white: the charger's status lights face the holder, and the ranger's power light comes off.

### Brass, aluminium and black

Brass treads and frame; bare aluminium drums; steel motor cans; black head, screen, rangers, charger, holder and speaker. White text on the screen, a white flashlight, one warm beacon. No green, blue, red or purple in sight, no card, no sheet, no paint, nothing printed. Prototype, not product: the head pulls off its horn, the firmware updates over the air.

### A camera, a microphone and an 8×8 ranger in the head

The [controller board](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C69FFVHH) is the head: one 21 × 17.8 × 15 mm stack with the camera and a digital microphone, its boards black. Pimoroni's black [8×8 laser ranger](https://shop.sb-components.co.uk/products/vl53l5cx-8x8-time-of-flight-tof-array-sensor-breakout) stands beside it: two eyes on a black face 41 mm wide. Both sit on a cradle of two 1 mm brass rods on the servo's horn, lifted 1.5 mm so the head turns clear of the taller screen. Ten silicone wires join the head to the frame in a loose loop: they twist rather than bend through its 120°.

### A voice: the owned speaker and amplifier

The microphone is on the camera board already. The voice is a spare [enclosed speaker](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/same-sky-formerly-cui-devices/CES-20134-088PM/10821309), black, lying on the spine behind the head and firing up, driven by a spare [I²S amplifier](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CDWXZZCH) hung under the motors. The controller sends it sound on three pins (D6, D7, D10), down three more neck wires and through the right rail. It draws about 120 mA while it speaks, so the treads wait while it talks (and while it listens, because their patter fills the microphone). Nothing to buy.

### A motor controller on the same two wires

The [motor controller](https://www.pololu.com/product/5079) runs motors from 1.8 V, so one cell drives it directly. It takes speed commands over I²C, stops both tracks if the commands stop (a 200 ms timeout), ramps acceleration, and reports the cell's voltage. Its board is blue, so it hangs under the motors.

### The head turns on a servo on the spine

The [servo](https://www.pololu.com/product/3420) stands shaft up on the two rails, glued between the axle in front and the back bar behind. The head's cradle sits on its horn: 120° on the datasheet's pulses, about 180° on wider ones. It is rated 4.8 to 6 V, so it runs on the black charger's 5 V boost, which the switch turns off with everything else.

### The laser ranger is the rover's hazard eye

The black [ranger](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09JLKKW1H) hangs on a brass stub on the axle, left of the screen, tilted 30° down. Its centre ray meets the desk 4.6 cm in front of the rover, 5.4 cm away: past its 4 cm minimum, about half a second ahead at full speed. When the desk disappears ahead, the tracks stop. It is the only sensor that sees a drop, so reversing is limited to 2 cm.

### An 8×8 ranger in the head, for the room

It reads 64 distances at once over a 45° square, 2 cm to 4 m. The head's 120° turn sweeps it, so one sweep sees about 165° ahead: walls, chair legs, the far side of the desk. The frames go to the desktop with the pictures, and the desktop builds a rough map of the room. It draws about 95 mA while it ranges and sleeps when the rover is parked.

### The drone's flashlight, as a headlight

The same [cool-white LED](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00LWU5DWM) on a star sawn to 14 mm, the same [10 mm lens](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/carclo-technical-plastics/10412/2641658), and one [350 mA regulator](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CST7GZTN) whose supply pin is the controller's pin D1: on, off and dimming with no MOSFET. Its tab is soldered to the brass axle, a better heatsink than Rev J's heat-shrink. About 140 lm in a 23° spot, right of the screen, pointing straight ahead; capped at 50 % in firmware.

### A beacon that blinks on the roof

The satellite's [pre-wired warm-white chip](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09QGJ9LY7), glued flat on the head's roof, 100 ms every 2 s through 100 Ω. It says the rover is on, and it turns with the head.

### One cell, where the RTG goes

The owned [16340 cell](https://imrbatteries.com/products/nitecore-nl169-16340-950mah-battery) in its black [holder](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CRGK889F) lies across the rear deck on the two motor cans. Behind it stands the [black charger](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/adafruit-industries-llc/6106/25805552), port up; in front, the owned [toggle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CZ6XZ2P9), lever to the right for on. Plug in with the switch off: about 2.2 h to full. Off grounds the rover's supply, which also turns off the charger's 5 V boost, so nothing drains the cell. About 1.9 h exploring with the camera streaming, 1.4 h with the flashlight on, 3.1 h stopped and talking, 5.4 h parked and watching.

### A reference, not a copy

Huy Vector's spy tank is a copper-wire box on hand-soldered metal treads, with three cells, a toy RC receiver, an analog FPV camera and two LED headlights on a resistor. This one keeps the idea: a palm-sized tracked chassis on a metal frame, treads of soldered rods, a camera, a light, a 16340 and USB-C charging. Everything else is its own: a wide little tank on a brass spine with a low turning head that sees, hears and speaks, laser-cut drums of two halves, metal loops instead of thread, Wi-Fi to a phone, a laser ranger that keeps it on the desk, one cell instead of three, brass and black instead of copper.

I²C: laser ranger `0x29` · display `0x3D or 0x3C, by the jumper on its back` · motor controller `0x10, plus the general call 0x00` · 8×8 ranger, on its own bus (D8, D9) `0x29, the laser ranger's address too: hence the second bus`.

Motor controller pins used: `VIN` and its `GND` (the cell after the switch), `M1A` `M1B` (left motor), `M2A` `M2B` (right motor), `VDD` (3.3 V from the head), `SDA`, `SCL`, one logic `GND`. Not wired: `VM`, `ERR`, `RST`, `JMP1`.

## What the firmware needs

- ESP-IDF 6.0.2 for the esp32s3, the family's toolchain. The `esp32-camera` component streams JPEG to a page served by the controller; the phone's browser shows the picture, a drive pad, a head slider, the lamp, and a Scan button.
- I²C at 400 kHz: ST's driver for the ranger (short mode, 50 Hz), the display through `esp_lcd`'s driver for its chip, and the motor controller's protocol from Pololu's user's guide: command timeout 200 ms, a gentle acceleration limit, speeds as signed numbers, the cell's voltage read once a second.
- A second I²C bus on D8 and D9 at 1 MHz for the 8×8 ranger: ST's ULD driver loads its firmware at boot, then 8 × 8 zones at 15 Hz while exploring, asleep when parked. Each frame carries the head's angle, so a sweep from −60° to +60° sees about 165° ahead; the frames stream to the desktop with the pictures, and the desktop builds a rough map of the room and sends back where to go.
- LEDC: the servo on D0 at 50 Hz (900 to 2100 µs, the head's 120°), down neck wire 6; the lamp on D1, a few kHz, set on the bench so the picture shows no banding, duty capped at 50 %; the beacon on D3, 100 ms every 2 s.
- Safety, in this order: the ranger reading the desk ahead every 20 ms while the tracks move forward, stopping and backing 2 cm when the reading jumps past the desk's distance; the 8×8 ranger's middle rows stopping it for anything within 10 cm ahead; reverse limited to 2 cm; the motor controller's own timeout; the cell below 3.4 V parks everything but the beacon.
- Sound, Rev K: the Sense board's microphone on I²S0 as PDM (clock GPIO42, data GPIO41), 16 kHz; the amplifier on I²S1, 16 kHz 16-bit mono out on D7 (bit clock), D6 (word clock) and D10 (data). The console moves to the USB Serial/JTAG port so UART0's two pins are free. The treads stop while it listens (their patter fills the microphone) and while it speaks (the current). The desktop that already runs the Pocket AI's server can be its voice: microphone frames up the Wi-Fi, speech back down.
- The screen: the distance ahead as a bar, the cell's percentage, the Wi-Fi name and address, and a line of text from the phone.
- Over-the-air updates after the first flash on the bench. The servo's 5 V comes on with the switch; the firmware waits half a second after boot before it moves the head, because the charger's boost will not start under a heavy load.

## Before power, with the meter

- Polarity, once: the holder's pigtail plug against the charger's socket with the cell in, button top to the pin the board marks +, before the plug goes in.
- Supply, once: switch on, the head not yet connected, the cell's voltage between neck wires 1 and 2 at their free ends, and 5.0 V on the charger's 5V pad before the servo's red lead goes on it; switch off, both read 0.
- The frame is ground: with the cell out, the frame to GND reads 0 Ω and the frame to BAT+ is open. Nothing but grounds touches a tube bare: BAT+ and every signal stay in silicone, inside the rails and out, and each rail's two open ends are filed smooth before the wires go in.
- Isolation, once: each motor's can to each of its two tabs reads open, because the cans sit on the grounded rungs; and each speaker lead to the frame reads open, because the amplifier's outputs are bridged.

- **A brass frame.** You asked for brass tubes. The two idler screws and their washers are gone. The rails carry the long wires inside.
- **No paint, no skins.** The rule of 2026-11-09. The skins and the painted drum faces are gone; the drums are bare aluminium.
