# 4.3 inches fills the visor; 7 does not

> Deep-research report written 2026-10-31 for the builder's question "can we use a much bigger screen, up to the whole 165 × 92 mm front?" The research notes behind it are in `../../research_notes/Bigger screen for Cyber Dawn/`. Decision pending; the power and purchase tables assume the solar-assisted design, which the builder is considering dropping for USB-C charging with a 16340 cell as backup.

**Buy Elecrow's 4.3 inch [advanced ESP32-S3 display board](https://www.amazon.com/ELECROW-ESP32-Display-4-3-Advanced/dp/B0G3WCDFLD) and keep the hand-wave gating.** Its 95 × 54 mm picture is 2.3× the owned OLED's 60 × 37 mm and sits inside the 60 mm visor zone with 3 mm to spare; it is IPS, 800 × 480, has an I2S amplifier and an I2C header on board, and its backlight dims over I2C in 245 steps, which the cheaper Waveshare boards cannot do at all ([Elecrow wiki](https://elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_4.3-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html)). The runner-up is the same maker's 5 inch [advanced display board](https://www.amazon.com/ELECROW-Interaction-Dual-Core-Processor-Compatible/dp/B0G3WC3LR2), whose 108 × 65 mm picture runs 5 mm below the top of the 32 mm base and so forces the visor and base to merge into one front. Every 7 inch panel is 97–100 mm tall as a module and fails the 92 mm face outright, which settles the size question before taste does ([DWIN datasheet](https://hubtronics.in/docs/LN80480T070IA9098.pdf), [Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-7B)). The price of any colour panel is pins and power: a 16-bit RGB panel takes 20 GPIO plus backlight and touch, so the display board replaces the owned controller board and the decided pin map is rewritten around the three spare pins the board leaves ([Elecrow wiki](https://elecrow.com/wiki/ESP32_Display-7.0_inch%28Advance_Series%29wiki.html)), and the controller cannot sleep while an RGB panel exists, so an always-visible 4.3 inch face costs about 9–35 Wh/day against a 9.6 Wh/day solar budget ([ESP-IDF source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/esp-idf/master/components/esp_lcd/rgb/esp_lcd_panel_rgb.c)). Presence-gated like today's OLED it costs about 1.3–4.6 Wh/day and the solar sail still makes sense; always on, the clock becomes a USB-C appliance with the 6600 mAh pack as an alarm reserve. Keep the already-bought panel as a free bonus, but stop sizing anything to it. All prices are 2026-10-31 vendor-direct figures; no Amazon buy-box price could be read.

## Only 4.3 and 5 inch modules fit the 92 mm face

The clock front is 165 × 92 mm with a 32 mm service pod at the bottom, so the visor zone is 60 mm tall. The table puts every size class against both limits; picture sizes come from glass datasheets and module sizes from the vendor pages, none measured ([4.3 inch glass](https://www.directindustry.com/prod/shenzhen-hongguang-display-co-ltd/product-4594500-2649795.html), [5 inch glass](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/291/NHD_5_0_800480TF_ATXL-248138.pdf), [Waveshare 4.3](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3), [Waveshare 5](https://www.waveshare.com/esp32-s3-touch-lcd-5.htm), [LCDwiki 4.0](https://www.lcdwiki.com/4.0inch_SPI_Module_ST7796), [LCDwiki 3.5 IPS](https://www.lcdwiki.com/3.5inch_IPS_SPI_Module_ST7796), [Newhaven 5.5 OLED](https://newhavendisplay.com/5-5-inch-green-graphic-oled-module/), [Waveshare 7.5 e-paper](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/7.5inch_e-Paper_HAT_Manual)).

**4.3 inches is the largest picture that stays inside the visor zone.** Centred in the 60 mm, the 53.9 mm picture leaves 3 mm top and bottom, and the glass border dips about 4 mm behind the top lip of the base, which the visor housing already covers in the current drawing. The board behind the glass is 68–75 mm tall on the Waveshare and Makerfabs versions; Elecrow does not publish an outline, so expect the same class and check the box before cutting acrylic ([Waveshare 4.3](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3), [Makerfabs 4.3](https://wiki.makerfabs.com/MaTouch_S3_Parallel_4.3_TFT_with_Touch.html)).

**5 inches means the base and visor become one front.** The 64.8 mm picture is 5 mm taller than the zone, and the 127.7 × 87.5 mm Waveshare board fills 92 mm of height to within 4.5 mm, so the battery, speaker and charger move behind the screen instead of under it. That is a different enclosure, not a taller one; the 72 mm depth absorbs it, but the drawn base-with-lid layout is gone ([Waveshare store](https://www.waveshare.com/esp32-s3-touch-lcd-5.htm)).

**7 inches fails on numbers, not feel.** All 7 inch glass is 164.9 mm wide and 97–100 mm tall with a 154 × 86 mm picture, so the frame would need a 165 × 104 mm cavity with zero side clearance, and the touch-equipped Waveshare boards are 193 × 111 mm ([DWIN](https://hubtronics.in/docs/LN80480T070IA9098.pdf), [Adafruit 7 inch panel](https://www.adafruit.com/product/2353), [Waveshare 7B](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-7B)). The 7.5 inch e-paper is the only panel whose picture truly fills the face, and it is 6 mm too tall, invisible in the dark with no factory front light at that size, and rated for one refresh per 180 s ([Waveshare wiki](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/7.5inch_e-Paper_HAT_Manual)).

## A colour panel costs 20 GPIO, so the display board replaces the controller board

**Every 4.3, 5 and 7 inch colour board uses the same 16-bit RGB bus: 16 data lines plus four sync lines, then a backlight line and three for touch.** On a bare controller board that is 24 of the 29 usable pins, and the six left over include four strapping pins and the on-board LED pin ([ESP-IDF RGB driver](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/lcd/rgb_lcd.html), [DevKitC user guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dev-kits/en/latest/esp32s3/esp32-s3-devkitc-1/user_guide_v1.1.html)). The decided pin map needs I2S on 14/15/16, amplifier shutdown on 17, snooze on 18, arm on 21, the alarm wire on 4 and five more; none of that survives. No vendor sells an adapter from the controller board's headers to a 40-pin panel, and the one hobbyist who wired it by hand then designed a PCB ([Hackaday.io](https://hackaday.io/project/185831/log/207217)).

**So at 4.3 or 5 inches the realistic part is a board with the ESP32-S3 already on its back, and the owned controller board becomes a spare.** The question is then which board leaves the clock's I2C chain, I2S amplifier and a few inputs free. The table is from vendor pin tables, not measurement ([Elecrow Advance 4.3](https://elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_4.3-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html), [Elecrow Advance 5.0](https://media-cdn.elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_5.0-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html), [Waveshare 4.3](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3), [Waveshare 5](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-5), [Elecrow 5.0 basic](https://devices.esphome.io/devices/elecrow-5inch-esp32-display/), [Sunton 4.3](https://www.espboards.dev/esp32/cyd-esp32-8048s043/), [Sunton 5.0](https://www.espboards.dev/esp32/cyd-esp32-8048s050/)).

| Board | I2C for the STEMMA chain | I2S amplifier | Spare GPIO | Backlight dimming | Verdict for this clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elecrow advanced 4.3 / 5.0 | yes, IO15/16 header (shared with touch 0x5D, RTC 0x51, helper 0x30) | yes, on-board amp on IO4/5/6 (give up the SD slot) | about 3: IO19, IO20, IO2 if the mic is unused | yes, one I2C byte to the helper: 0 = max, 244 = min, 245 = off (V1.2+) | meets; add an I2C expander for the leftover switches |
| Elecrow 5.0 basic | IO19/20, shared with touch | likely IO17/18/42 as on the 7.0 basic, unverified | unverified | true PWM on GPIO2 at 1220 Hz; one forum report of it not working on V3 | probable, but 4 MB flash and unverified pins |
| Waveshare 4.3 / 4.3B / 5 / 5B | yes, GPIO8/9 header (shared with touch and expander 0x24) | none | GPIO6, maybe 43/44 | on/off only through the I/O expander | fails: no audio, no night dimming |

| Makerfabs 4.3 | one connector | none stated | 1 | needs a resistor soldered on | fails |

**The clock's I2C chain has no address clash with either maker.** The chain sits at 0x68, 0x49, 0x60, 0x18, 0x38 and 0x36; Elecrow reserves 0x5D, 0x51 and 0x30, Waveshare 0x20–0x27, 0x30–0x3F and 0x5D ([Waveshare 4.3](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3), [Elecrow](https://elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_4.3-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html)). On the Elecrow board the three spare pins all lie in the 0–21 range that can wake the chip from deep sleep, so the alarm wire, the proximity interrupt and snooze take them; the arm switch, the encoder interrupt, the gauge alert and charger status move to an I2C GPIO expander on the chain, which the notes name as the standard rescue for pin-starved boards ([espboards.dev](https://www.espboards.dev/esp32/elecrow-crowpanel-advance-7-esp32-s3/)). Keep the owned [real-time clock](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5188): the board's own clock chip has no alarm wire to the controller.

**Firmware is the built-in ESP-IDF RGB driver plus the official touch component; no registry display driver is needed.** Espressif's best-tested RGB case is exactly 800 × 480 at a 21 MHz pixel clock, 26 fps average in LVGL 8, with the 768 KB frame buffer in the 8 MB octal PSRAM ([Espressif LCD FAQ](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/espressif-esp-faq/en/latest/software-framework/peripherals/lcd.html)). The known failure is image drift when Wi-Fi or a flash write starves the DMA, and the documented cure is 10-line bounce buffers, restart-in-VSYNC, XIP from PSRAM and a modest pixel clock ([ESP-IDF RGB driver](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32s3/api-reference/peripherals/lcd/rgb_lcd.html), [atomic14](https://www.atomic14.com/esp32/boards/sunton-esp32-8048s070/)). Elecrow lists ESP-IDF among its platforms and all vendor demos are LVGL 8, so LVGL 9 is a porting job on any board ([Elecrow wiki](https://elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_4.3-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html)).

**Keeping the controller board is possible only over SPI.** A 4.0 inch [SPI display module](https://www.slashskill.com/deals/product/B0CKRJ81B5/hosyond-4-0-inches-480x320-tft-touch-screen-lcd-display-module-spi-st7796s-drive/) at $19.99 has an 83.5 × 55.7 mm picture, fits the visor zone within a millimetre, runs on the already-decided GPIO 12/11/10/6/7 plus one PWM pin, and has an official ESP-IDF component for its controller ([LCDwiki](https://www.lcdwiki.com/4.0inch_SPI_Module_ST7796), [component registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_lcd_st7796/versions/1.4.0/readme)). Its catch is the panel: the capacitive version is TN and the resistive one does not state its type, and the only IPS module in the family is the 3.5 inch at 73 × 49 mm ([LCDwiki 4.0 capacitive](https://lcdwiki.com/4.0inch_Capacitive_SPI_Module_ST7796), [LCDwiki 3.5 IPS](https://www.lcdwiki.com/3.5inch_IPS_SPI_Module_ST7796)). The other SPI route, Adafruit's [graphics-controller board](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1590) at $39.95 driving a bare 4.3 or 5 inch panel over five wires, works in Arduino but has no official ESP-IDF path for SPI and forum reports of artifacts, so it is not a fit for this ESP-IDF firmware ([component registry](https://components.espressif.com/components/espressif/esp_lcd_ra8875), [LVGL forum](https://forum.lvgl.io/t/ra8875-esp32s3-artifacts-and-strange-offsets/9904)).

## An RGB panel keeps the controller awake, so always-on costs 9–35 Wh/day

**The ESP-IDF RGB driver takes a no-light-sleep power lock when the panel is created and releases it only when the panel is deleted.** An RGB panel has no frame memory, so the controller, its PSRAM and the panel's drivers stream 768 KB thirty to forty times a second for as long as the picture exists; a dim always-on face is therefore a fully awake controller all night ([esp_lcd_panel_rgb.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/esp-idf/master/components/esp_lcd/rgb/esp_lcd_panel_rgb.c)). The owned OLED and any SPI module hold their own picture, which is why the current design can blank and sleep.

**The only bench measurement of an RGB board is Adafruit's 4 inch square-panel board: about 170 mA running with the backlight, 8 mA with the backlight off, under 1 mA in deep sleep** ([Adafruit Learn](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-qualia-esp32-s3-for-rgb666-displays/deep-sleep)). Waveshare rates its 4.3 inch board at 450 mA on 5 V, and 5 inch glass is rated at 1.0–1.3 W of backlight, which is 235–305 mA on the 5 V rail through an 85 % boost ([Waveshare 4.3](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4.3), [Texim 5 inch panel](https://catalogue.texim-europe.com/product/display-and-touch-solutions/display-panels/tft-lcd-display/detail/tst050wvbs-51-tsd)). The streaming floor with the backlight off, derived in the notes from vendor totals and a 30 mA bare-board measurement, is 60–200 mA ([lucidar.me](https://lucidar.me/en/esp32/power-consumption-of-esp32-s3-devkitm-1/), [GUITION](https://www.guition.com/knowledge/how-to-power-a-7-inch-esp32s3-display-module)).

The table applies those inputs to the three ways the clock could use a screen, using the project's own conversion of 1 mA continuous on the 5 V rail = 0.141 Wh/day from the cell. These are arithmetic from vendor ratings, not measurements, and the 4.3 inch backlight at minimum is a guess of 5–30 % duty because no vendor publishes a dimming floor.

| Usage model | 4.3 inch RGB board | 5 inch RGB board | 4.0 inch SPI module (3.5 inch figures, ± 10 %) | Owned OLED (project estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Visible all night, dimmed | 65–245 mA → **9–35 Wh/day** | 70–290 mA → **10–41 Wh/day** | 2.3–4.5 Wh/day screen + 0.3–4.2 controller | 3.96 Wh/day at 50 mA |
| B. Hand-wave gated, 1.2 h/day on, deep sleep | **1.3–4.6 Wh/day** | **2.3–4.9 Wh/day** | 1.0–1.35 Wh/day | less than A |
| C. Visible all day at full brightness | 170–450 mA → 24–63 Wh/day | 300–500 mA → 42–70 Wh/day | 20–24 Wh/day | 3.96 Wh/day |

Sources for the SPI and OLED columns: [Arduino forum 3.5 inch](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/power-save-9486-tft-with-esp32/701118), [mischianti 2.8 inch board](https://mischianti.org/?p=32414), [LCDwiki 4.0 backlight 103 mA](https://www.lcdwiki.com/4.0inch_SPI_Module_ST7796), the project's [solar feasibility note](SOLAR_FEASIBILITY.md).

**Model A breaks the 9.6 Wh/day budget on every RGB size, even in its most optimistic corner.** Model B fits with room to spare and is exactly what the OLED does today, so the big screen changes nothing about the energy plan as long as it stays dark until a hand appears. Model A on the SPI module is the one always-visible option within budget, because the controller can light-sleep behind a static picture while the PWM keeps running ([ESP-IDF sleep modes](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/esp32s3/api-reference/system/sleep_modes.html)).

**A boot-time load of 170–450 mA is also the charger board's weak spot.** The project's own feasibility note warns that the [charger board](https://www.adafruit.com/product/6106) can fail to cold-start from a weak panel if a load above about 200 mA appears suddenly; with an RGB board the display must be brought up after the rail settles, not at power-on ([solar feasibility note](SOLAR_FEASIBILITY.md)).

## Backlights leak, so dim to a few per cent and keep the hand-wave

**No backlit panel goes truly black; the owned OLED is still the best thing in the room at 3 a.m.** A clock builder who tried every hobby display concluded that "all the hobby displays I've passed through my hands have had a severe backlight problem, except OLED", and IPS glow at the corners is "inherent to the technology" ([dotview-clock](https://github.com/jef-sure/dotview-clock), [BenQ](https://www.benq.com/en-us/support/faq/0122015.html)). A 4.3 inch picture is 2.3× the OLED's lit area and the backlight leaks across all of it, so a visible-all-night TFT needs the dimmest setting the driver allows, red or amber digits on black, and nothing else lit ([TFT_clock](https://github.com/nlimper/TFT_clock)).

**The dimming floor is the spec to check, and it is why the Waveshare boards lose.** Their backlight is an on/off line on an I/O expander; the one working PWM hack on the 7 inch sibling needed a wire soldered to a pad and its author set a 30 % minimum, which on a 4.3 inch board is still tens of milliamps of light ([Home Assistant thread](https://community.home-assistant.io/t/esp32-s3-7inch-capacitive-touch-display-adjust-brightness/771030), [Spotpear forum](https://spotpear.com/index.php/ESP32-S3N8R8-7inch-LCD-Display-TouchScreen-800x480-LVGL-CAN-Sensor-RS485/forum-answer/275.html)). Elecrow's advanced boards expose 245 steps over I2C and the basic 5.0 a true 1220 Hz PWM, both vendor claims; whether the lowest steps are usable in a dark bedroom is unmeasured, and users of commercial clocks call 5 % "too strong" ([Elecrow](https://elecrow.com/wiki/CrowPanel_Advance_4.3-HMI_ESP32_AI_Display.html), [ESPHome device page](https://devices.esphome.io/devices/elecrow-5inch-esp32-display/), [Garmin forums](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation-archive/f/enduro/290617/in-dark-conditions-5-background-light-is-too-strong-why-not-add-1-2-3-4)).

**Two side effects belong in the design, not the surprise list.** PWM below about 100–200 Hz flickers and many backlights misbehave under roughly 5 % duty, and a 1 kHz PWM into a boost-type LED driver can make its ceramic capacitors sing within earshot of the I2S amplifier on the same 5 V rail; the cure is a PWM above 20 kHz or analogue dimming where the driver allows it ([LKML](https://lkml.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/1009.1/00535.html), [Analog Devices](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/technical-articles/2023/05/03/23/15/how-to-reduce-pwm-related-acoustic-noise-on-max20444-applications.html), [TI E2E](https://e2e.ti.com/support/power-management-group/power-management/f/power-management-forum/1388367/tps923653-issues-with-tps923653-led-driver-audible-noise-and-excessive-ringing-with-pwm-dimming)). The upside of a TFT is that burn-in disappears: the OLED's blanking was partly a lifetime measure, and a TFT can show a static face for years ([NHD datasheet](https://newhavendisplay.com/content/specs/NHD-5.5-25664UCG3.pdf)).

**The practical night mode is the one already designed: dark until a hand, then a dim amber face for a minute.** That sidesteps glow, the dimming floor and the power lock in one move, and it is the only configuration in which a 4.3 or 5 inch RGB board meets the energy budget.

## Keep the solar sail as a bonus; design for USB-C

**Drop the panel from the design, not from the parts bin.** The project's own verdict already names USB-C the dependable primary and the 5 W panel a window-facing assist worth about 6 Wh/day at two peak-sun hours and 12 Wh/day at four, so the panel never carried an always-on colour screen at any size ([solar feasibility note](SOLAR_FEASIBILITY.md)). With a presence-gated 4.3 inch board at 1.3–4.6 Wh/day the sail still covers the screen on a fair day; with a visible-all-night face at 9–35 Wh/day it covers a third to nothing, and the 6600 mAh pack's 16.6 Wh of usable energy is 11–44 h of reserve.

**So the decision follows the usage model, not the screen.** If the screen stays dark until a hand, keep the solar plan unchanged: the panel is bought, the [charger board](https://www.adafruit.com/product/6106) accepts USB, DC and solar on one input, and nothing else needs to be ordered. If the point of a big screen is to read it from the pillow without waving, the clock is a USB-C appliance with a battery that exists to ring the alarm through an outage, and the panel stays in the drawer or hangs in the window as an experiment. Either way nothing is re-bought, and the 6 h charge-timer limit on the charger board matters less once the wall is the primary source.

## Recommendation and runner-up

**Buy the Elecrow 4.3 inch [advanced ESP32-S3 display board](https://www.amazon.com/ELECROW-ESP32-Display-4-3-Advanced/dp/B0G3WCDFLD), and keep the clock dark until a hand appears.** It is the only 4.3 inch board that is IPS, dimmable, carries an I2S amplifier and exposes an I2C header, and its picture is the largest that fits the drawn 60 mm visor without touching the base layout. Order it as V1.2 or later (the I2C dimming register is revision-dependent), budget one I2C GPIO expander for the arm switch and the pollable status lines, and plan the firmware as the built-in RGB driver with bounce buffers at a 16–21 MHz pixel clock, the panel deleted before every sleep. The owned controller board becomes the spare; the [real-time clock](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5188), [amplifier](https://www.adafruit.com/product/3006) and the whole sensor chain carry over unchanged, and the on-board amplifier can even replace the owned one.

**Runner-up: the Elecrow 5 inch [advanced display board](https://www.amazon.com/ELECROW-Interaction-Dual-Core-Processor-Compatible/dp/B0G3WC3LR2), identical electrically, 46 % more picture, and a new enclosure.** Choose it only if the drawn base-and-visor form is negotiable, because the 108 × 65 mm picture and the roughly 130 × 85 mm board turn the front into one screen with the battery behind it. Its 0.135 mm pixels are coarser than the 4.3 inch's 0.119 mm but the digits are physically taller, which is what matters from a pillow.

**If the firmware and wiring must not move, the 4.0 inch [SPI display module](https://www.slashskill.com/deals/product/B0CKRJ81B5/hosyond-4-0-inches-480x320-tft-touch-screen-lcd-display-module-spi-st7796s-drive/) at $19.99 is the honest third option.** It doubles the picture, uses the decided pins plus one, keeps the controller asleep behind a static face, and is the only candidate that can stay visible all night inside the solar budget; the cost is a non-IPS panel seen from an angle.

## Conclusion

The size decision was never about taste: 7 inch glass is 97–100 mm tall and the face is 92 mm, so the builder's instinct that 7 feels too large is simply the enclosure talking. What the research changes is the mental model of a colour screen on this chip. An RGB panel is not a bigger OLED; it is a peripheral that keeps the controller streaming a frame buffer for as long as it exists, so "dim it at night" is not an option, "turn it off until a hand" is, and the hand-wave gating designed for OLED burn-in turns out to be the thing that keeps the solar plan alive.

That also reframes the solar question. The panel was never going to carry a lit screen of any size; it carries a dark clock that wakes for a minute at a time, and it still does with a 4.3 inch board. The only scenario that retires it is the one where the big screen is read without waving, and in that scenario the right move is to call the clock a USB-C appliance, not to buy a bigger panel.

## Vendor claims, measurements and gaps

**Measured:** the 4 inch RGB board's 170 mA on, 8 mA backlight-off and under 1 mA asleep (Adafruit bench); a bare controller board's 30 mA active and 0.93 mA deep sleep (lucidar); a 3.5 inch SPI module's 175 mA with 150 mA of it backlight (Arduino forum); a 2.8 inch board's 115 mA (mischianti); PWM dimming confirmed working by two users on the Waveshare 7 inch. **Vendor claims:** every dimension, picture size and outline; Waveshare's 450 mA and 350 mA board ratings; all backlight currents and brightness figures; Elecrow's dimming register and pin tables; all prices. **Derived here:** every Wh/day figure, the 5–30 % dimming-floor bracket, and the fit margins.

**Gaps:** no Amazon buy-box price could be read for any listing, so Prime status and street price are unknown; Elecrow publishes neither a price nor an outline for the advanced 4.3 and 5 inch boards; the 5 inch basic board's I2S and spare pins are unverified; no 4.3 inch glass backlight current was found; no bench current exists for any 4.3, 5 or 7 inch ESP32-S3 board at full, dimmed or sleeping; the Elecrow helper's lowest usable brightness, PWM frequency and deep-sleep behaviour are undocumented; Waveshare does not list its boards' unused pins; the SPI path through Adafruit's graphics-controller board has no official ESP-IDF support; and the 4.0 inch resistive module's panel type is unstated.
