Desk Robot Crumb, the final build as proposed

A square brass-rod case with a flat vertical face, riding on two Ø40 mm steel 64-tooth spur gears as wheels, the way Mo-chan does, on one shared axle. One 1.54" colour IPS screen (240 × 240) fills the lower face; a brow above it carries the camera lens and the 8×8 ranger, and the edge ranger lies flat under the top rail looking down past the nose. A brass dome skid carries the nose. The cell lies across the floor just ahead of the motors, which sit low on the side walls at axle height; the speaker fills the tail wall, and the amp, fuel gauge and I²C motor driver stand on the walls above the motors. All three drawings are to one scale.

Envelope 72 W × 56 D × 60 H mm (cage 52 wide; gears add 10 each side) · wheels Ø40, axle 20, inline · ground clearance 6 · edge ranger reads the desk 59 mm away, 21 mm ahead of the skid

What the real gear changed, later the same night

FoundFixed
The Globact listing's own drawing gives the 64T gear as Ø40, 9 mm over its boss, 5 mm bore: a 0.6 module gear. The design had assumed 0.8 module, Ø52.8, 5 mm thick. All three listings are the same part.Axle 26.4 → 20. Motors down the side walls to y 15–25. Speed 13 cm/s unloaded, about 10 under load.
At y 15–25 the motors ran through the cell holder lying front-to-back on the floor.Holder turned across the body on the floor just ahead of the motors (z 0–18, x ±21.5). IMU moved from the nose floor to the tail floor.
9 mm of gear against a 10 mm N20 shaft.Boss inboard, 1 mm off the cage; hub 9 mm long; the shaft reaches 8 mm into it. 72 over the gears instead of 66.
Gears lighter than assumed: the pack of one gear and four pinions ships at 50 g.About 32 g per gear with hub; total mass about 210 g.

Open on the hardware, not on paper: which end of the Sense board carries the camera (decides whether the USB-C port faces up), Wi-Fi strength inside the brass cage, the gear's real diameter, plate thickness and boss length (Ø40, 9 mm overall and a 5 mm bore per the listing's drawing), and the stall current of the actual motors, which sets the firmware's drive cap. Steel teeth can slip on glass or laminate; a desk mat fixes that.
