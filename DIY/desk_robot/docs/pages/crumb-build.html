Crumb Rev C: the brass cage, without the lidar

72 × 56 × 60 mm, about 182 g: Crumb with the lidar off the roof. On 2026-11-09 the builder found the lidar too big and asked for it off both desk robots, so they stay small and cute. The lidar, its posts on the top rails, its 5 V switch and its serial line are gone, and the top comes down 35 mm to the cage’s own rail. Everything else is Rev B’s, and nothing is left to buy.

Millimetres · x right, y up from the desk, z forward · front, side (face right) and top (face up) · lighter = on the far wall · dashed = hidden · orange dashes the ranger’s cone

What it is

A cage of 1.0 mm brass rod, 52 wide, 56 deep and 54 tall with r 3 corners, open on every face. Two N20 motors lie end to end on one axle 20 mm up and 7 mm back, and Crumb’s two 64-tooth steel gears ride on their shafts outside the posts, each with an ivory brass disc on its face so the steel shows only as a toothed rim. The 1.54" square screen fills the lower face in an ivory bezel; the XIAO ESP32-S3 Sense stands behind it in an ivory sleeve with its lens on the brow, and the edge ranger sits beside the lens looking 40° down at the desk ahead.

Inside: the cell holder across the floor with the 16340 cell in it, the charger-boost flat on the tail floor with its USB-C out the back, the motor driver on the left wall, the amplifier and fuel gauge on the right, the motion sensor on the tail floor, the speaker as the tail wall and the toggle beside it. Nothing sits on the roof: the top is the cage’s own rail.

Brass is the cage, the hubs and the skid, its own colour. Ivory is every skin: the brass sheet cut to each board, Kapton inside, canvas white outside. Black only in the screen’s glass, the lens and the speaker; steel only as the gears’ teeth; white wire along the rods. The builder skipped the brass sheet on 2026-11-08: the first build runs bare, its boards’ red, blue, green and black showing through the cage. The drawings show the finished look.

Inside, and the stance

Every part is an axis-aligned box the script checks against every other and against the cage; nothing overlaps and nothing leaves it, so nothing rises above the top rail at 60 mm. The centre of mass lands 5.5 mm ahead of the axle and 24 mm up, 11 mm lower than Rev B’s, so Crumb rests on its nose skid with about 32 g on it. Braking pitches it harder onto the skid and cannot lift the gears below 10.3 m/s²; accelerating forward would lift the skid above 2.2 m/s². The firmware ramps at 0.4.

Every wire, pin to pin

Rev B’s sheet less the lidar’s branch. One cell, one toggle, one boost, one brain. The cell rail (red) runs from the toggle through the fuel gauge into the charger-boost, and straight to the motor driver. The 5 V rail (brass) comes off the charger-boost’s pads and feeds the XIAO through a Schottky and the amplifier, nothing else. Everything else is 3.3 V from the XIAO on three buses: SPI to the screen, I²S to the amp, and one I²C chain through the gauge, the driver, the ranger and the motion sensor. D2 and D3, the lidar’s two pins, are free.

From the cell: about 0.53 A roaming with the camera streaming, so about 1.8 h on the full 950 mAh (Rev B, with the lidar spinning: about 1 h). Parked with the face on and the camera off, 0.21 A, about 4.6 h. Take a tenth off both for a real cell. The boost carries 250 mA typical and 630 peak of its 1 A, a margin Rev B did not have.

A full charge at 500 mA takes about 2.2 h with the toggle on, since the toggle sits in the cell’s lead; Crumb keeps running on the lead. Speed: about 60 rpm at the cell’s voltage, 45 under load, 9 cm/s on Ø40; it stops in about 13 mm from that, against the 43 mm the ranger gives it.

What the firmware needs

  • Boot order: Wi-Fi up, screen on, chain scanned (0x36, 0x5D, 0x29, 0x68); D2 and D3 set as inputs with pull-downs.
  • Safety loop at 50 Hz: ranger over 110 mm or no target → brake, back 15 mm, turn; ranger under 70 mm (something standing in its cone) → stop, turn; motion sensor pitch or roll over 5° in 50 ms, or a bump → brake.
  • Drive: ramps of 0.4 m/s², the cap from the stall test, turns in place about the axle, reversing limited to 15 mm because nothing looks back.
  • Face: Pip’s eyes at 240 × 240 on the square screen (ST7789, 26 MHz), backlight PWM with a night floor and a parked-off state. Sound on I²S1, cap 50. Double tap on the body = the button.
  • Sleep: camera off, Wi-Fi on, the face dimmed; the gauge’s state of charge on the face.

Build order

  1. Bench tests on jumpers first: the chain scan (0x36, 0x5D, 0x29, 0x68), the screen, the amp, the motors from the bench supply at 3.7 V with a 0.5 A limit (stall current sets the drive cap).
  2. Caliper the two gears (outer diameter, bore, plate, length over the boss); correct the numbers at the top of this generator and regenerate before any brass is cut.
  3. Hubs: 9 mm of 5 mm tube over 9 mm of 4 mm tube, soldered, pressed into each gear with the boss inboard; true each gear on its motor shaft (1 mm of wobble at the rim is a waddle); squeeze the inner tube into the shaft's flat so it drives.
  4. Skins, when the sheet is bought (skipped 2026-11-08): cut each piece from the brass sheet to the table below, line the inside with Kapton, spray the outside canvas white, two coats, dry overnight; the two gear faces the same, with a Ø5 hole for the hub. The first build goes straight to the cage with bare boards.
  5. Bend and solder the cage: four posts, eight rails, r 3 corners; then the two fender rails at y 14.5 with the motor cradles, the brow sill, the skid post with its dome. Nothing goes on the top rails.
  6. Motors on their cradles, shafts out; gears on; the body must sit level on both gears and the skid.
  7. Floor: the holder across the front, the charger-boost at the tail with its USB-C between the back rods, the motion sensor on the right tail floor. Walls: the driver left, the amp and gauge right. Tail: the speaker and the toggle. Face: the screen in its bezel, the XIAO in its sleeve behind it, the ranger on the brow right of the lens.
  8. Kapton on every board back toward brass; wire to the sheet in white; meter every rod and every skin against every net; then the checks above in order.
  9. First roll on a towel on the floor, not the desk.

Risks

RiskWhat is done about it
The boost's margin250 mA typical and 630 peak of its 1 A, with the amp loud and Wi-Fi sending. Rev B ran 560 and 950 with the lidar; the margin is back. If the rail ever dips, cap the amp first.
The charger-boost under the motorsFlat on the tail floor it passes 0.9 mm under the motors only without its screw terminal; the 5 V pair solders to the terminal's pads instead. If the board comes with the block soldered, two pins to unsolder.
Nothing looks far aheadWith the lidar gone the edge ranger is the only range sensor. Its cone sweeps the desk 40–112 mm ahead of the face; anything standing in it, a mug or a book, reads shorter than the desk and stops Crumb as an edge does. A pen lying flat may not; the motion sensor feels the bump. The camera sees further when the server is on. Aimed along a desk, one zone mostly reads the desk at its near foot, so this ranger cannot pick out a low object 30 cm away: the camera and the server can, at a few frames a second, and an 8×8 ranger on the brow could, which is why one is on the list to buy, just in case (2026-11-10).
Edges alongside, and behindHead-on, the steep ray meets the desk 43 mm before the skid and 74 mm before the wheels, and Crumb stops in 13 mm from 9 cm/s. But the foot is only 34 mm wide where it first meets the desk and sits 13 mm right of the midline, while the wheels run 31.5 mm either side: an edge within about 66° of head-on is seen before a wheel reaches it (to 89° on the right, under the ranger), an edge running almost alongside is reached by the outer wheel first, the left sooner, and nothing looks back. Four rules for the drive loop close that with no new part: step at most 72 mm, then sweep ±45°, whose foot covers both wheel tracks 74–146 mm ahead; reverse only the 20 mm back-off after an edge stop, over desk just crossed; spin in place, never arc, near an edge; a missing or flagged reading is a stop (glass desks defeat the beam, so Crumb will refuse to move on one; sun gives false stops, which are safe). The last line is the motion sensor: if a wheel drops, the body rolls until the floor rail meets the desk edge at about 6°, the 5° tilt stop firing first, and the centre of mass stays over the other wheel and the skid, so Crumb hangs with one wheel in the air rather than falling. Worked through 2026-11-10.
Pushing small thingsTraction sets the push: about 150 g rides on the wheels, so steel teeth on wood give roughly 0.5 N and the wheels slip before the motors stall. That moves anything under about 105 g that slides, and heavier things that roll: a pen, an eraser, a bottle cap, a spare cell; not a mug, not a phone. Anything under about 27 mm tall within 20 mm of the face stays under the cone, so the desk beyond it is still read and the edge stop keeps working; in push mode a slightly shorter reading is the object's back at the near foot, a longer one is still the edge. Things that fill the foot, a book or a long box, blind it: not those. Push at half speed, which cuts the stop to a few millimetres so the object's far side stays on the desk. The screen module starts 7 mm above the desk and the only bumper today is the 6 mm floor rail and the skid, so pushing wants a bumper rod across the front posts at about 15 mm, a few millimetres proud of the face: one more line, for a Rev D, not drawn.
The nose skidThe centre of mass sits 5.5 mm ahead of the axle and 24 mm up, 11 mm lower than Rev B's with the lidar on the roof. It rests on its nose skid with about 32 g. Braking cannot tip it over the skid below 10.3 m/s²; accelerating forward lifts the skid only above 2.2. The firmware ramps at 0.4.
Gear on hub on shaftThree fits in series, and Crumb's recipe wanted a drop of CA, which the bench does not have. The 4 mm tube's bore is the shaft's 3 mm: press it on and squeeze a dimple into the D-flat with pliers so it drives; the 5 mm tube soldered over it fills the gear's bore, and the boss's grub screw, if the gear has one, clamps the stack (else a bead of hot glue on the inboard end). True each wheel on its motor before the cage closes.
Steel teeth on the desk64 tooth tips a turn tick at about 48 a second at speed, faint on wood, sharper on glass, and they can mark a soft finish. Grip is not in question: 0.4 m/s² needs a friction coefficient of 0.04. A desk mat, or a silicone O-ring stretched over the teeth, if the tick matters; the look says bare teeth.
The seller's Ø40The gear is drawn from the listing (Ø40, 9 over the boss, 5 mm bore; 39.6 by its module) and it is on the bench: caliper it before anything is cut. The axle height is its radius; the width is 2 × (27 + its length over the boss).
Skins in an open cageA skin is 0.13 mm brass over Kapton: it insulates by the Kapton, not by itself. Every skin is metered against every net before power, and no skin reaches a rod. Spray before fitting; keep the iron off the painted side.
Wi-Fi inside brassAn open cage of 1 mm rod is not a shield at 2.4 GHz; the antenna flex lies flat on the inside of the XIAO's sleeve, facing the open top. Compare RSSI against the bare board before the skins go on.
Two free pinsNine pins in use, two free (D2, D3). A double tap on the body, read by the motion sensor, is the button; the toggle is the only switch. The owned button could come back on D3, or the 8×8 ranger to buy on D2 (its LPn); neither is drawn.

Re-checked, 2026-11-09

Rev B’s electrics were checked on 2026-11-07 and 2026-11-08 against the Seeed wiki, the Adafruit 6106 and 5580 guides, the SparkFun motor driver page and the Pololu carrier page. Rev C only takes things away, so the re-check is of what the lidar touched: the pins, the power chain, the run time, the mass and the balance.

WhatHow
PinsNine header pins in use on nine different GPIOs (1, 2, 5–9, 43, 44), checked by the script; D2 (GPIO3) and D3 (GPIO4) are free. GPIO3 is a strapping pin only with the JTAG eFuse burned, which it is not, so it may float through boot now that its 100 kΩ is gone; the firmware makes both inputs with pull-downs. GPIO43 prints the ROM's boot log and drives only the screen's DC line; GPIO44 is the ROM's RX, pulled up, and drives only the backlight. I²S sits on GPIO 7, 8, 9, freed by cutting J3; the PDM microphone keeps I²S0, the amp takes I²S1.
I²C chainUnchanged. Four addresses, none shared: ranger 0x29, gauge 0x36, driver 0x5D, motion sensor 0x68. Pull-ups once the driver's jumper is cut: 2.2 kΩ ∥ 10 kΩ ∥ 10 kΩ = 1.5 kΩ, a 2.2 mA sink, under the 3 mA the bus allows at 400 kHz.
The power chainCell, toggle, fuel gauge, charger-boost, in that order, as Rev B. The 5 V rail feeds the XIAO through the Schottky and the amp: 250 mA typical, 630 peak of the boost's 1 A. Nothing new rides on the boost's start: the XIAO and the amp were on the rail at power-up in Rev B too, and the lidar came on a second later through its switch. Motors on the cell rail only: 150 mA running, 700 stalled, 1.4 A for two under the firmware's cap, inside the cell's 2 A protection. The toggle sits between the cell and the charger, so the cell charges with the toggle on.
Why the 5 V rail staysNothing on Crumb needs 5 V by itself: the XIAO has battery pads and the amp runs from 2.5 V. But on its battery pads the XIAO brings its own 100 mA charger onto the cell beside the charger-boost, makes the cell rail live whenever its USB is in with the toggle off, and sags with every motor stall at a low cell. Through its 5V pin and the Schottky it sits on a steady 4.7 V, the charger-boost is the only charger, and the toggle still kills everything. So the boost stays in use, for the XIAO and the amp.
The lidar's branch, removedGone: the lidar and its ZH1.5 pigtail, the three posts on the top rails, the P-MOSFET, the N-MOSFET that drove it, the 10 kΩ, 1 kΩ and 100 kΩ, the 100 µF and 100 nF at the lidar, the serial wire to D3 and the switch wire from D2. Nothing else on the sheet changed its connections; the amplifier moved up under the rail where the switch and the lidar were, and the screen's reset network was redrawn so no ground symbol sits on the clock line.
The diodeSS14 Schottky in the XIAO's 5V lead, 0.3 V drop at 0.3 A: the rail feeds the pin, a PC's USB never backfeeds the rail. Flash with the toggle off, or over the air.
ScreenST7789 on 4-wire SPI through the GPIO matrix at 26 MHz: CLK GPIO1, DIN GPIO2, DC GPIO43, CS tied to GND as the only SPI device, RST 10 kΩ to 3V3 with 1 µF, BLK on GPIO44 PWM; 3.3 V throughout. The header order (GND VCC SCL SDA RES DC CS BLK on this family) is read off the silkscreen before wiring.
Amp and speakerMAX98357A on 5 V with 220 µF at VIN, GAIN open (9 dB), SD on its own pull-up; BCLK, LRC, DIN on GPIO 7, 8, 9. Bridged output: neither speaker lead is ground.
Charger-boostbq25185: charge-rate jumper cut for 500 mA (0.53 C on the 950 mAh cell), EN open, six-hour timer, power path so the robot runs on the lead; BAT through the gauge's pass-through; the 5 V pair soldered to the pads of the unfitted terminal block. Its boost is in use.
GeometryThe overlap audit passes for every pair of parts and every part stays inside the cage: nothing rises above the top rail at 60 mm. The charger-boost clears the motors by 0.9 mm. The ranger's steepest ray meets the desk 71 mm away, past the sensor's 40 mm floor.
