## The ask (2026-11-07)

camera, a lidar, a small speaker and a microphone. Three prototype drawings first, then the pick, then the full

## Shared electronics (decisions of this round)

- **One 5 V rail from an Adafruit bq25185 charger + 5 V boost (6106):** USB-C in, charge 1 A default → **cut SJ1
  for 500 mA** (0.53 C on the 950 mAh cell; Nitecore's own NL169R charges from 5 V 1 A), TPS61023 boost 5 V / 1 A,
  EN pad (true disconnect), runs while charging (boost sits on the charger's power path), 29 × 19 mm, JST-PH cell
  lead. $8.95 Adafruit / DigiKey 1528-6106-ND; not on Amazon. Rejected: the owned PowerBoost 1000C copy (1 A fixed
  charge, micro-B, unverified), SparkFun 14411 (20 mA while charging), TP4056+boost generics (no load sharing).
- **The motors stay on the cell rail** through the Qwiic driver's VIN (3–11 V), so stall current never loads the
  boost. 5 V rail typical ≈ 600 mA, peak ≈ 950 mA.
- **The XIAO's 5V pin takes the rail through a Schottky** (Seeed's wiki: the 5V pin is VBUS, no diode on the board);
  BAT pads stay empty; flash over the XIAO's USB-C with the toggle off.
- **The lidar's 5 V goes through a high-side switch** (P-MOSFET to buy + owned AO3400A as its gate driver, 100 kΩ
  pull-down on the GPIO): the TPS61023 stalls if > 200 mA hangs on it at power-up, and the switch is also the lidar's
  sleep. Lidar TX (3.3 V, 230400 8N1, one-way) → UART1 RX on GPIO4. PWM pin left open (internal 10 Hz).
- **Pins (XIAO, J3 cut).** Mochi/Lantern: D0 SCK, D1 MOSI, D2 DC, D3 lidar RX, D4 SDA, D5 SCL, D6 (GPIO43) screen
  backlight PWM (boot chatter = a flicker), D7 (GPIO44) lidar switch, D8–D10 I²S to the amp; no button (IMU double
  tap). Wheelie: D0 lidar switch, D1 lidar PWM (optional), D2 button, D3 lidar RX, D4/D5 I²C (OLEDs 0x3C/0x3D), D6
  status light, D7 spare, D8–D10 I²S. Mic on I²S0, amp on I²S1, as Crumb.
- **I²C chain:** driver 0x5D, IMU 0x68, gauge 0x36, VL53L1X 0x29 (only one ST ranger now: no re-addressing), plus
  the two OLEDs in Wheelie. Remove the GY-521's and the Qwiic driver's 2.2 kΩ pull-ups (Crumb's bus note).
- **Run time:** ≈ 1.0 A from the cell roaming with the lidar on → about an hour; lidar only while moving → ≈ 1.5 h;
  parked, lidar and camera off → 4–5 h. Charge 2.2 h at 500 mA, robot may keep running.

## Research of 2026-11-07 (three agents; sources on the page)

- **Lidar.** LDROBOT STL-19P (Waveshare D500 kit, $95.99; Hiwonder $99.99; Amazon B0CNCLQ68L price not read): body
  38.59 × 38.59 × 33.5, rotor Ø35.29 × 12.6 on top, **three-lug flange 54.0 × 46.3 × 1.3 at the base** (3 × Ø2.5
  holes), 45 g, 4.5–5.5 V, 290 mA running / ~300 start, ZH1.5 4-pin (Tx, PWM, GND, 5V), 230400 8N1, TX 3.3 V,
  0.03–12 m, ±10 mm to 0.5 m, 5000 Hz, 10 Hz; scan plane ≈ 27 mm above the flange's underside, window ≈ 24.8–30.8.
  Same housing as the older LD19/D300 (180 mA, 4500 Hz). Runner-up YDLIDAR T-mini Plus (38.6 square, no flange,
  45 g, but 340 mA running, 840–1000 mA start, UART start/stop). Too big: RPLIDAR C1 (55.6 mm, 110 g), LD14P/D200
  (96 mm, 101 g). ESP32 library: kaiaai/LDS.
- **Wheels.** Pololu white wheels press onto the 3 mm D-shaft: 32×7 (#1088, Ø31 real, 3.1 g, $4.45/pair), 40×7
  (#1454, 4.3 g, $5.45), 60×8 (#1424, 11.3 g, $6.75; also DigiKey marketplace). White ABS hub, black silicone tyre.
  Not on Amazon in white; the only white Amazon N20 wheel (ALLPARTZ 43×19) has a 19 mm tyre.
- **Round screen.** Waveshare 1.28" GC9A01 (round Ø37.5 board, Ø32.4 picture, 8-pin cable with BL pin, 3.3/5 V):
  Amazon B08V5538C6 (price not read), Waveshare $14.99, PiShop $16.95. DIYables 1.28" $14.99 on Amazon: square
  38 × 38 board, no BL pin. esp_lcd has gc9a01 and gc9d01 components.
- **Power.** See above; Nitecore publishes no charge-current figure for the NL169 (Seiko protection IC; 2 A
  discharge); XIAO: SGM40567 charger 100 mA, SGM6029 buck 600 mA, 5V pin = VBUS.
