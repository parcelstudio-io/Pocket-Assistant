What you are looking at

  • Eyes first. While you are inside the camera's cone, only the pupils move: the firmware draws them where it sees your face. Nothing mechanical happens.
  • Then the sphere. When you drift past about 18° off the sphere's axis, the eyes are near the edge of the glass. That is the cue: the sphere turns to put you back in the middle, and the eyes slide back to centre as it does. A short pause before each turn keeps it from twitching.
  • Two axes, two servos. Pan is the fork turning on the servo in the coaster. Tilt is the sphere nodding on the servo that forms its right hinge. Both are sub-micro servos; the sphere's weight hangs below the tilt axis, so it levels itself when the power is off.
  • Limits. Pan stops at ±60° (the wires inside the left tine twist no further), tilt at ±25°. Beyond that the eyes just look as far as they can.
  • Blinks are the only motion that is not tracking.
